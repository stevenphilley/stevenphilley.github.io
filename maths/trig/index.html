Unit circle

How to read it. Each spoke is an angle from the positive x-axis. Radians sit inside the circle, degrees sit just outside the spoke, and the point (cos θ, sin θ) sits further out, in exact form.

Quadrant I triangles are the 30°, 45°, and 60° reference triangles. Sine of 30°, 45°, 60° runs 1/2, √2/2, √3/2. Cosine runs that pattern backwards. Other angles use the same reference values with a sign change.

Check yourself

A short SOHCAHTOA / identity check. On the unit circle the hypotenuse is 1, so sin θ is the y-coordinate and cos θ is the x-coordinate.

Useful identities

Pythagorean: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Every point on this circle is exactly 1 from the origin.

Tangent: tan θ = sin θ / cos θ when cos θ ≠ 0. That is why tan is undefined at 90° and 270°.

Cofunction: sin(90° − θ) = cos θ, and cos(90° − θ) = sin θ. That is the 30° / 60° swap.

Accretion and
the Eddington limit

A star or a black hole can swallow mass. The light that comes out is L = ε Ṁ c². ε is the fraction of the rest-mass energy that escapes as radiation. If the light pushes back as hard as gravity pulls, the flow sits at the Eddington luminosity. For a sphere of pure ionized hydrogen, with Thomson scattering as the only opacity, that limit is LEdd = 4π G M mp c / σT. It scales with mass. Slide the mass from one solar mass to ten billion. The chart is log luminosity against log mass. The holes themselves, as radii, are the black-hole desk. A remnant that is not a hole is a compact star. The radioactive light after an explosion is supernovae.

Educational calculator. Not a disk model and not a catalog. The Eddington luminosity is 4π G M m_p c / σ_T for a spherical flow of pure ionized hydrogen, with Thomson scattering as the only opacity. That opacity is the electron-scattering limit in Rybicki and Lightman. σ_T, m_p, and G are 2022 CODATA central values. c is exact. L = ε Ṁ c², the accretion luminosity in Frank, King, and Raine. The e-folding time is ε/(1−ε) × σ_T c / (4π G m_p) / λ. Salpeter 1964 is the name on that clock. Real disks can exceed L_Edd. Super-Eddington flows, geometry, and radiation-pressure effects are not in these formulas. Named masses are cited central values and are labeled approximate. The 100 to 10⁹ solar-mass span is a classroom seed, not a named quasar.

The Sun button is one nominal solar mass. L☉ is the IAU 2015 nominal luminosity, 3.828 × 10²⁶ W. It is a conversion constant, not a new measurement of the Sun. The point of the button is the gap: that wattage sits far under L_Edd. The Sun is not accreting at the Eddington limit. The marker on the chart at 1 L☉ is the nominal luminosity. The marker on the line is the limit.

Efficiency ε is the fraction of rest-mass energy that comes out as light, L = ε Ṁ c². The default is 0.1. The Schwarzschild ISCO binding energy is 1 − √(8/9), which is 0.0571910. The maximal prograde Kerr closed form used here is 1 − 1/√3, which is 0.422650. Familiar round figures are 0.057 and 0.42. 0.0571910 rounds to 0.0572 at three significant figures and to 0.057 at two. It does not round to 0.057 at three figures. 0.422650 rounds to 0.423 at three significant figures and to 0.42 at two. The buttons use the binding energies. 0.057 and 0.42 are not substituted. This page does not integrate a Kerr geodesic.

The e-folding time is ε/(1−ε) × σ_T c / (4π G m_p) / λ. At these sliders it is 5.00545 × 10⁷ Julian years. The product that drops (1−ε) and keeps only ε is 4.50490 × 10⁷ years. A familiar round figure is about 45 million years, from that shorter product at ε = 0.1 and λ = 1. The calculator uses ε/(1−ε). The two times are not averaged. The mass that stays is (1−ε) of the supply, which is why (1−ε) is in the denominator of the growth rate. Salpeter 1964 is the name on the clock.

The seed is 100 solar masses and the target is 10⁹. The number of e-folds is ln(10⁷). At the efficiency and Eddington ratio on the sliders, the time is that count times the e-folding time. This span is the classroom example. It is not a named quasar. Changing ε or λ changes the time and does not change the e-folds.

Above the line

The sphere is the limit. A disk is not the sphere.

L_Edd here is the luminosity where radiation force balances gravity for a spherical flow of pure ionized hydrogen. The only opacity is Thomson scattering, σT / mp. Rybicki and Lightman treat that electron-scattering limit. A real accretion flow is not a sphere. Frank, King, and Raine is the textbook for disks and for L = ε Ṁ c². A disk can exceed the spherical limit. The usual names are super-Eddington accretion, a different geometry, and radiation-pressure effects. This page does not compute any of them. λ above 1 is only a number on the slider.

The efficiency buttons are binding energies at the innermost stable orbit, not a fit to a named disk. For a non-spinning hole the orbit is at 6M and the binding energy is 1 − √(8/9). For a maximal prograde Kerr hole the closed form used here is 1 − 1/√3. Bardeen, Press, and Teukolsky 1972 give the orbits. This page does not integrate a Kerr geodesic, and it does not assign a spin to Cygnus X-1, Sagittarius A*, or M87*.

Literacy · the numbers on the readout

Eddington luminosity
LEdd = 4π G M mp c / σT. Gravity pulls on a proton. Thomson scattering pushes on the electron that travels with it. Set the two forces equal and the luminosity falls out proportional to M. One nominal solar mass computes 1.25707 × 10³¹ W, which is 3.28387 × 10⁴ L☉. A familiar round figure is 1.26 × 10³¹ W. 1.25707 × 10³¹ rounds to 1.26 × 10³¹ at three figures. The calculator uses the arithmetic. A familiar round figure near 3.2 × 10⁴ L☉ is coarser. 3.28387 × 10⁴ rounds to 3.28 × 10⁴ at three figures. 3.2 × 10⁴ is not substituted.
Accretion luminosity
L = ε Ṁ c². Ṁ is the rest-mass supply rate. ε is the fraction that comes out as light. At Eddington ratio λ the light is λ LEdd, so Ṁ = λ LEdd / (ε c²). The default ε is 0.1.
Eddington rate
ṀEdd = LEdd / (ε c²). The readout’s Ṁ is that rate times λ, in kg/s and in solar masses per Julian year. At 1 M☉, ε = 0.1, and λ = 1 the arithmetic is 2.21980 × 10⁻⁸ M☉/yr.
Salpeter time
The mass that stays is (1 − ε) of the supply, because the radiated fraction left. The e-folding time is t = ε/(1−ε) × σT c / (4π G mp) / λ. At ε = 0.1 and λ = 1 the arithmetic is 5.00545 × 10⁷ Julian years. The product that drops (1−ε) and keeps only ε is 4.50490 × 10⁷ years. A familiar round figure is about 45 million years, from that shorter product. The calculator uses ε/(1−ε). The two times are not averaged. Salpeter 1964 is the name on the clock. The time does not depend on the mass. A bigger hole has a bigger limit and a bigger supply, in the same proportion.
e-folds
From a seed mass to a higher target, the count is ln(target / seed). The time is that count times the e-folding time. 100 to 10⁹ is ln(10⁷) = 16.1181 e-folds. At ε = 0.1 and λ = 1 the time is 8.06783 × 10⁸ years. The span is a classroom example. It is not a named quasar.
The Sun
L☉ is the IAU 2015 nominal luminosity, 3.828 × 10²⁶ W. L☉ / LEdd at one solar mass is 3.04519 × 10⁻⁵. The Sun is not accreting at the limit. The button is the gap.

Sources · the constants and the masses

Cited, not invented

  • NIST SP 961, CODATA 2022 — Thomson cross section σe = 6.6524587051 × 10−29 m², standard uncertainty 0.0000000062 × 10−29. The uncertainty is not folded in. The same adjustment’s proton mass and G are linked below.
  • NIST — proton mass — 2022 CODATA central value, 1.67262192595 × 10−27 kg. The same value as the star-formation desk. Pure ionized hydrogen uses one proton per electron.
  • NIST — Newtonian constant of gravitation — 2022 CODATA G = 6.67430 × 10−11 m³ kg−1 s−2. c = 299,792,458 m/s is exact. A year on this page is the Julian year, 365.25 × 86,400 s.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B3 (Prša et al. 2016, AJ 152, 41) — nominal GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 10²⁰ m³ s−2 and nominal solar luminosity 3.828 × 10²⁶ W. A solar mass is GM☉ / G. L☉ is the comparison wattage, not a new measurement of the Sun.
  • Rybicki and Lightman, Radiative Processes in Astrophysics (Wiley, 1979) — Thomson scattering and the electron-scattering opacity. This page uses σT / mp. It does not copy a page number.
  • Frank, King, and Raine, Accretion Power in Astrophysics (Cambridge, 3rd ed., 2002) — the textbook treatment of L = ε Ṁ c² and of accretion onto compact objects and active nuclei. This page does not copy an equation number, and it does not solve a disk.
  • Salpeter 1964, ApJ 140, 796 — accretion of interstellar matter by massive objects. The e-folding clock on this page carries his name. The formula used here is ε/(1−ε) times σT c / (4π G mp λ). The product that drops (1−ε) is shown beside it and is not substituted.
  • Bardeen, Press, and Teukolsky 1972, ApJ 178, 347 — photon orbit and ISCO for a Kerr hole. The zero-spin ISCO is 6M. This page’s efficiencies are the binding energies 1 − √(8/9) and 1 − 1/√3. It does not evaluate their Kerr formula.
  • Miller-Jones et al. 2021, Science 371, 1046 — Cygnus X-1, 21.2 ± 2.2 M☉. The same central value as the black-hole desk. The ±2.2 is not folded in. Also arXiv:2102.09091.
  • GRAVITY Collaboration 2022, A&A 657, L12 — Sagittarius A*, (4.297 ± 0.012) × 10⁶ M☉ statistical, systematics about 40,000 M☉. The preset uses 4.297 × 10⁶. A round 4.3 × 10⁶ is not substituted.
  • EHT Collaboration 2019, ApJL 875, L6 — M87* Paper VI, 6.5 ± 0.2 (stat) ± 0.7 (sys) × 10⁹ M☉. The preset uses 6.5 × 10⁹.

Star formation

A cold molecular cloud collapses when gravity beats thermal pressure. For a uniform sphere the boundary used here is the virial line 2K = |U|. The gravitational energy is (3/5) G M²/R. The thermal energy is (3/2) (M / (μ mH)) k T. Put the radius in terms of density, ρ = n μ mH, and the critical mass is the Jeans mass on this page: MJ = (5 k T / (G μ mH))3/2 × (3 / (4 π ρ))1/2. The Jeans length here is the critical radius of that same sphere, RJ = (15 k T / (4 π G μ mH ρ))1/2. If pressure is then ignored, the free-fall time is tff = (3 π / (32 G ρ))1/2. At 10 K and 104 particles per cm3, with μ = 2.33, the arithmetic is 5.37577 solar masses, 0.130599 pc, and 337.100 thousand years. Other textbooks change the numerical factor. A wavelength form is named below and is not substituted. The four buttons are typical textbook values, not measurements of a named cloud. How that mass then lives on the main sequence is the stellar lifetime desk. Where the star sits in temperature and luminosity is the HR diagram. The nearest of those stars are the nearest stars.

Classroom uniform-sphere picture, not a simulation of a cloud. Rotation, magnetic fields, and turbulence are left out. n is the number of particles per cubic centimetre. μ is the mean mass of one particle in units of mH, so ρ = n μ mH. The default μ = 2.33 is a round textbook value for cold molecular gas. It already includes molecular hydrogen and helium. It is not a measured abundance, and it is not a second factor on top of an H2-only count. G is the 2022 CODATA central value, 6.67430 × 10−11 m3 kg−1 s−2. k is exact, 1.380649 × 10−23 J/K. mH is the proton mass plus the electron mass, 1.67353286432 × 10−27 kg. The 13.6 eV binding energy is omitted. A solar mass is the IAU 2015 nominal GM☉, 1.3271244 × 1020 m3 s−2, divided by that G, which is 1.9884098707 × 1030 kg. One parsec is (648000/π) au, 3.085677581491 × 1016 m. A year is the Julian year, 31,557,600 s. Parenthetical CODATA uncertainties are not folded in.

What the number is

One form of the Jeans criterion. Set 2K = |U| for a uniform sphere and replace R with (3M / (4πρ))1/3. The mass that sits on that line is MJ = (5 k T / (G μ mH))3/2 × (3 / (4 π ρ))1/2. Rice University’s ASTR 350 notes of 28 October 2021 print this mass and call RJ the Jeans length. Simon Fraser University’s PHYS 390 notes print the same mass and name Carroll and Ostlie as the text. A wave analysis often writes a Jeans length (π cs² / (G ρ))1/2 with cs² = k T / (μ mH). That length is longer than RJ by 2π / √15, which is 1 / 0.616404. This page computes it and does not substitute it. The two are not averaged. A cloud heavier than MJ, or larger than RJ, is the unstable side of this particular line. Real clouds also rotate, carry magnetic fields, and are turbulent. Those terms are not in the formula.

The notes’ 29, written beside this arithmetic. University of Toronto AST 320 notes print MJ = 29 solar masses × μ−2 × (T / 10 K)3/2 × (n / 104 cm−3)−1/2, and RJ = 0.30 pc × μ−1 × (T / 10 K)1/2 × (n / 104 cm−3)−1/2. At μ = 1, T = 10 K, and n = 104 per cm3, this page’s arithmetic is 29.1845 solar masses and 0.304295 pc. 29.1845 rounds to 29 at the integer the notes print. 0.304295 rounds to 0.30 at two decimals. The extra digits stay. They are not averaged. The notes do not print the constants behind 29. The same notes call tff = (3π / (32 G ρ))1/2 the exact free-fall time, beside √(R³ / GM) and ∼ 1/√(Gρ). This page uses the exact form. The other two are not substituted.

Fragmentation, and why cooling matters. At fixed temperature and μ, MJ falls as n−1/2. The log-log plot is a straight line of slope −1/2. An isothermal collapse therefore crosses the Jeans line in smaller pieces as the density rises, so one cloud can make many stars. NASA Science says batches of stars that have recently formed from molecular clouds are often called stellar clusters, and that molecular clouds full of those clusters are stellar nurseries. If the gas cannot radiate the heat of compression, T rises. MJ grows as T3/2. For an adiabatic ideal gas with γ = 5/3, the Rice notes point out that MJ then rises as the density rises, and fragmentation stalls. Molecular cooling is what keeps a cold cloud near 10 K long enough for stellar-mass pieces. The free-fall time does not depend on T. It is the time for a pressureless uniform sphere, released from rest, to fall to a point. A cloud below MJ does not freely fall. NASA Science also says molecular clouds range from 1,000 to 10 million times the mass of the Sun and can span as much as hundreds of light-years. That range is NASA’s description of the clouds. It is not a Jeans mass from this formula. They are not averaged.

What the words mean

Jeans mass
The uniform-sphere mass at which 2K = |U|. Heavier than this, at the same density and temperature, gravity wins on this line. Lighter, thermal pressure wins. The prefactor is the one written above.
Jeans length
R_J, the radius of the sphere whose mass is M_J. Rice’s notes call this radius the Jeans length. The wavelength form is a different length and is not used as the answer.
Free-fall time
t_ff = (3π / (32 G ρ))1/2. It depends on density only. It is the pressureless collapse time, not a guarantee that a supported cloud will collapse.
μ
Mean mass per particle, in units of m_H. ρ = n μ m_H, and n counts particles. 2.33 is the molecular default. 1.3 is the neutral atomic button. 0.6 is the ionized button, and the electrons count as particles.
Fragmentation
M_J shrinks as the density rises, if the gas stays cold. Pieces of the cloud become unstable on their own. The outcome NASA describes is a stellar cluster, not one star from the whole cloud.
Cooling
Compression heats a gas that cannot radiate. M_J then climbs as T3/2, and for γ = 5/3 it climbs as the density climbs too. Molecular lines and dust keep a cold cloud near 10 K, which is why the dense-core button is a few solar masses and the warm H II button is not.

Sources

Where the formula and the constants come from

  • Rice University, ASTR 350 lecture notes, 28 October 2021 — the uniform-sphere Jeans mass MJ = (5kT / (G μ mH))3/2 (3 / (4πρ))1/2, and RJ = (15kT / (4π G μ mH ρ))1/2, which those notes call the Jeans length. The notes also say that for γ = 5/3 the Jeans mass rises as the density rises in an adiabatic collapse. This page uses that form. It does not adopt the notes’ example tildes.
  • University of Toronto, AST 320 notes — the same mass, printed as 29 solar masses × μ−2 × (T / 10 K)3/2 × (n / 104 cm−3)−1/2, and RJ = 0.30 pc on the same scaling. The exact free-fall time is (3π / (32 G ρ))1/2. This page’s μ = 1 arithmetic is 29.1845 solar masses and 0.304295 pc. They are not averaged. The file is ast320notes.pdf.
  • David Boal, Simon Fraser University, PHYS 390 Lecture 25 — the same Jeans mass, with the text named as Carroll and Ostlie, sections 12.1 and 12.2. The copyright line on the notes is 2001. This page does not use that lecture’s round constants.
  • NASA Science — Star basics — stars form in molecular clouds. The page says those clouds range from 1,000 to 10 million times the mass of the Sun and can span as much as hundreds of light-years, that cold gas clumps, and that gravity causes some clumps to collapse. Batches of stars from those clouds are often called stellar clusters. The mass range is NASA’s. It is not this formula.
  • NIST — Boltzmann constant — k = 1.380649 × 10−23 J/K, exact.
  • NIST — Newtonian constant of gravitation — 2022 CODATA recommended value, 6.67430 × 10−11 m3 kg−1 s−2, standard uncertainty 0.00015 × 10−11. The uncertainty is not folded in. In solar masses the Jeans mass still depends on G, because MJ / M☉ scales as G−1/2.
  • NIST — proton mass — 2022 CODATA central value, 1.67262192595 × 10−27 kg. The parenthetical standard uncertainty is not folded in.
  • NIST — electron mass — 2022 CODATA central value, 9.1093837139 × 10−31 kg. Added to the proton mass. The 13.6 eV binding energy is omitted. The sum used here is 1.67353286432 × 10−27 kg.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B3 (Prša et al. 2016, AJ 152, 41) — nominal solar mass parameter GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 1020 m3 s−2. Divided by the CODATA G it is 1.9884098707 × 1030 kg. The same constant as the stellar lifetime desk’s nominal luminosity paper, and as the rotation-curve desk.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — the astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 m.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B2 — footnote 4: the parsec is exactly (648000/π) au. This page’s value of that product is 3.085677581491 × 1016 m. A year in the free-fall time is the Julian year, 365.25 × 86,400 s = 31,557,600 s.

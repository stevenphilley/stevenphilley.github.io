Sections · Resonances

Vol. Science · Astronomy · The ratio

The same
meeting point

A mean-motion resonance is a lock between two orbital periods. Write p:q when the inner body completes p orbits in the time the outer body completes q. JPL states the 3:1 Kirkwood gap that way: the asteroid completes 3 orbits for every 1 of Jupiter. Kepler’s third law turns that period ratio into a distance. For one central mass, a₂/a₁ = (P₂/P₁)2/3. The Kepler desk is the one-sun form, P² = a³ in years and AU. This desk anchors the period and the semi-major axis to a fact sheet, then applies the ratio. Jupiter’s sheet, the anchor for the belt, prints 5.20336301 AU and 4,332.589 days. At an exact ratio, conjunctions return to the same longitudes, so the gravitational nudges keep landing in the same places.

Circular, coplanar, and Keplerian. A p:q ratio means the inner body completes p orbits while the outer body completes q. The resonant period is the anchor period times that ratio. The resonant semi-major axis is the anchor axis times the period ratio to the power 2/3, which is Kepler’s third law for one central mass. Fact-sheet periods are not replaced by that integer ratio. Where both periods are printed, the card shows the printed ratio beside p/q. The drawing of an exact ratio keeps conjunctions on fixed rays. A drawing that uses printed periods lets the meeting point walk. JPL’s June 2007 histogram shows the Kirkwood gaps. This page marks the computed axes and does not replot those 156,929 orbits. Not an ephemeris, not a proper-element catalog, and not a three-body integration.

Jupiter’s fact sheet prints a semimajor axis of 5.20336301 AU and a sidereal orbit period of 4,332.589 days. A 3:1 ratio means the inner body completes 3 orbits while the outer body completes 1. The resonant body is interior, so its period is the anchor period times 1/3, which computes 1,444.196 days (3.9539 years of 365.256 days). Its semi-major axis is the anchor axis times (1/3) to the power 2/3, which computes 2.501516 AU. That is Kepler’s third law for one central mass: a₂/a₁ = (P₂/P₁) to the power 2/3. An exact 3:1 meets at 2 longitudes. The drawing keeps the ratio exact, so every conjunction returns to those places. What the words mean

p:q
The inner body completes p orbits while the outer body completes q. Mean motions are the other way up, because a shorter period is a faster mean motion. JPL’s 3:1 sentence is the asteroid completing three orbits per Jupiter orbit, so p = 3 and q = 1.
Distance from the ratio
a₂/a₁ = (P₂/P₁) to the power 2/3. The anchor’s period and semi-major axis stay the fact-sheet numbers. Only the ratio is applied. A year on the card, when days are divided out, is Earth’s 365.256-day sidereal period as printed on these sheets.
Conjunction
The two bodies share a longitude, so they and the central mass lie on one line. For an exact p:q in lowest terms, those meetings repeat at p − q fixed longitudes. A 2:1 has one. A 3:1 has two, opposite each other. The nudge at conjunction does not average away.
Kirkwood gaps
Depletions in the asteroid belt at Jupiter’s 3:1, 5:2, 7:3, and 2:1. JPL’s June 2007 histogram shows them and says the resonance changes the orbital elements enough to open a gap in semi-major axis. The chart marks the computed axes.
Hilda group
Bodies that stay near Jupiter’s 3:2, interior to Jupiter and outside the 2:1 gap. The computed axis is 3.970909 AU. Ip’s 1976 study, in the NASA Technical Reports Server abstract, finds that 3:2 coupling stable for some of them. JPL’s elements diagram labels the group. This page does not replot it.
Laplace and a near resonance
NASA Science describes Ganymede, Europa, and Io as one orbit, two orbits, and four. The Jovian Satellite Fact Sheet periods are 1.769138, 3.551181, and 7.154553 days. The pairwise ratios are close to 2 and 4 and are not those integers. The combination (n_Io − 3 n_Europa + 2 n_Ganymede) / n_Io equals 1 − 3 (P_Io/P_Europa) + 2 (P_Io/P_Ganymede). The card computes it. TRAPPIST-1 periods are Agol et al. 2021, Table 2. Adjacent ratios are computed here and compared with the nearest small integers. Agol and coauthors credit Luger et al. 2017 with the chain of generalized Laplace relations. A near ratio lets the meeting point walk. An exact ratio pins it.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

  • NASA — Basics of Space Flight, Kepler’s third law — the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis. This page uses a₂/a₁ = (P₂/P₁) to the power 2/3. The same statement is on the Kepler desk.
  • NASA NSSDCA planetary fact sheets — Semimajor axis (AU) and sidereal orbit period, the same lines as the Kepler desk. Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Earth’s 365.256 days is the year unit when a day count is divided out. Jupiter is 5.20336301 AU and 4,332.589 days. Mars is 1.52366231 AU. Neptune is 30.06896348 AU and 60,189.018 days.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Pluto Fact Sheet — Semimajor axis (AU) 39.48168677. Sidereal orbit period 90,560 days.
  • NASA Science — Neptune facts — for every three laps Neptune takes, Pluto makes two. That repeating pattern prevents close approaches.
  • NASA Science — Kuiper Belt facts — Pluto is in a 3:2 resonance with Neptune: it circles the Sun twice for every three times Neptune goes around. The same page names the plutinos. The map of that belt, with this 3:2 drawn at 39.401489 AU, is the dwarf planets desk.
  • JPL SSD — Asteroid main-belt distribution — histogram of June 2007, 156,929 numbered asteroids with well-determined orbits. Gaps labeled 3:1, 5:2, 7:3, and 2:1. The 3:1 is where the asteroid’s period over Jupiter’s is 3/1. This page does not replot the counts.
  • JPL SSD — Asteroid elements distribution — June 2007 diagram, 159,366 numbered asteroids. Labeled groups include the Hilda group, between the main belt and the Trojans. This page does not replot it.
  • NASA NTRS 19770033109 — Ip, 1976, a dynamical study of the Hilda asteroids. The abstract says the 3:2 resonant coupling to Jupiter of some of these small asteroids is stable.
  • NASA Science — Asteroids — most asteroids orbit the Sun between Mars and Jupiter, in the main asteroid belt. That sentence is the span of the chart. It is not a pair of sharp edges.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Jovian Satellite Fact Sheet — Io 1.769138 days and 421.8 × 10³ km. Europa 3.551181 days and 671.1 × 10³ km. Ganymede 7.154553 days and 1,070.4 × 10³ km.
  • NASA Science — Ganymede facts — every time Ganymede orbits once, Europa orbits twice and Io orbits four times. The fact-sheet periods are the numbers this page divides. The one-two-four sentence is the rounded pattern.
  • Agol et al. 2021, PSJ 2, 1 — TRAPPIST-1 Table 2 central periods, b through h: 1.510826, 2.421937, 4.049219, 6.101013, 9.207540, 12.352446, and 18.772866 days, with the 1σ uncertainties in the table on this page. Adjacent pairs lie near mean-motion resonances. The chain of generalized three-body Laplace relations is credited there to Luger et al. 2017. No semi-major axis from that table is loaded.

How this desk works

p is how many orbits the inner body finishes. q is how many the outer body finishes in the same time. The axis follows from the periods.

Controls

What each control does
BodyThe fact-sheet anchor. Its printed period and semi-major axis stay fixed. The ratio is applied to them.
p and qWhole numbers from 1 through 16, with p greater than q. 4:2 is read as 2:1. The boxes are for a solar orbit. A moon or TRAPPIST-1 button uses printed periods instead, and the boxes wait until you edit them or pick a belt button.
InteriorThe resonant body is inside the anchor. Kirkwood gaps and the Hildas are interior to Jupiter. Period = anchor period times q/p.
ExteriorThe resonant body is outside the anchor. Neptune–Pluto is this case: Neptune is inner, Pluto is outer. Period = anchor period times p/q.
PlayPause or play. The faster body takes about eight seconds per orbit. If your system asks for less motion, the drawing starts still.
RaysSolid rays are an exact p:q, and the bodies stay on them. Dashed rays are the nearest exact ratio when the bodies are moving at printed periods. The meeting point then walks off the ray.
Belt chartMars to Jupiter. The five marks are computed. It is not JPL’s histogram. A solar solution inside that span is marked too.

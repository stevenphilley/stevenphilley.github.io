Cosmology
several distances

A redshift is not yet a distance. The same z is an age, a lookback time, a comoving ruler, a luminosity distance, and an angular-diameter distance. They are different integrals of one expansion history. Slide z from 0 to 20, or type a higher one. The default model is flat Planck 2018, Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing: H0 = 67.36, Ωm = 0.3153, ΩΛ = 0.6847. A second button sets the SH0ES Hubble constant, 73.04, and keeps those densities, because that paper measures H0 and not Ωm. Radiation is left out. The wavelength shift is the redshift desk. One redshift, several distances

The ruler today, the faintness, and the angle are not the same length.

Comoving distance is the ruler you would lay down today between here and a galaxy that stayed in the Hubble flow. It only grows. Luminosity distance is the answer to “how far would this have to be, in a static Euclidean space, to look this faint?” Photon energy drops as 1/(1+z), and photons arrive less often by another 1/(1+z), so it is (1+z) times the transverse comoving distance. A standard candle is fainter than the comoving ruler alone would say.

Angular-diameter distance converts an angle on the sky into a proper size at the source, the size the object had when the light left. It is the transverse comoving distance divided by (1+z). The comoving piece grows with z. The division by (1+z) grows too. Past a redshift of about 1.6 in this flat model, the division wins, and the angular-diameter distance turns over. A more distant galaxy can subtend a larger angle than a nearer one of the same proper size. That peak is on the chart. It moves if you change Ωm or H0.

Lookback time is how long the light has been traveling. The age at redshift z is how old the universe was when the light left. Their sum is the age today. A lookback of 8 Gyr is 8 billion light-years of light-travel distance, because a Julian year of light is one light-year. That light-travel distance is not the comoving distance. The comoving distance is longer: space stretched while the photon was en route. cz/H0 matches the comoving distance only while z is small. The distance ladder stops at that toy. This page is the integral.

Literacy · the words on the readout

E(z)
Hogg’s equation 14: E(z) = √[Ωm(1+z)³ + Ωk(1+z)² + ΩΛ]. H(z) = H0 E(z). Ωk = 1 − Ωm − ΩΛ. There is no radiation term. A photon’s flight and a clock both come from dividing by E(z). Flat means Ωk = 0, so the transverse distance equals the line-of-sight comoving distance.
Age
The age today integrates dz/[(1+z)E(z)] from 0 to infinity, times 1/H0. The age at z stops the integral at that redshift and runs it to infinity. Planck’s published 13.797 ± 0.023 Gyr includes radiation. This page does not, and it prints 13.801 Gyr for the table’s central values.
Lookback
Age today minus age at z. Hogg’s equation 30. It is the light-travel time. In billion light-years of light-travel distance it is the same number. It is not a comoving distance.
Comoving
DC = (c/H0) ∫ dz/E(z). Hogg’s equation 15. The line-of-sight ruler today. At low z it sits near cz/H0. c = 299,792.458 km/s. For H0 = 67.36, c/H0 = 4450.6 Mpc.
Luminosity
DL = (1+z) DM. Hogg’s equation 21. DM is the transverse comoving distance, equation 16. In a flat universe DM = DC. This is the distance a magnitude uses when the source is cosmological. The distance ladder does not switch over to it.
Angular diameter
DA = DM/(1+z). Hogg’s equation 18. Proper size per radian at the time of emission. One arcsecond is π/(180×3600) radians, so the scale in kpc per arcsecond is DA in megaparsecs, times 1000, times that angle. The nearby version of the same angle, without redshift, is the angular size desk. Hogg notes that DA turns over instead of growing forever. For these Planck parameters the turnover is at z = 1.588.
Flat or curved
Flat locks ΩΛ = 1 − Ωm. Curved lets both densities move, and Ωk is whatever is left. Positive Ωk uses sinh. Negative Ωk uses sin. If that sine goes negative, the ray has passed the antipode of a closed universe and the distances stop being a simple ruler.
Gly
Billion light-years, using 3.2616 light-years per parsec, the same four-decimal rounding as the magnitude and distance-ladder desks. The gigayear is different: 10⁹ Julian years of 365.25 × 86400 seconds, with the IAU parsec, so that H0 becomes a time. Light-travel Gly equals lookback Gyr. Comoving Gly does not.

Sources · the parameters and the presets

Cited, not invented

  • Hogg 1999, “Distance measures in cosmology,” arXiv:astro-ph/9905116 — eq. 4, DH = c/H0; eq. 14, E(z), with no radiation; eq. 15, line-of-sight comoving distance; eq. 16, transverse comoving distance; eq. 18, angular-diameter distance; eq. 21, luminosity distance; eq. 30, lookback time. He writes that the linear Hubble law holds only at small redshift. The age is the same integral as the lookback, run from z out to infinity.
  • Planck Collaboration 2020, A&A 641, A6 (arXiv:1807.06209) — Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing, 68% limits. Central values used here: H0 = 67.36 ± 0.54 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3153 ± 0.0073, ΩΛ = 0.6847 ± 0.0073, age 13.797 ± 0.023 Gyr, z* = 1089.92 ± 0.25. The abstract rounds H0 to 67.4 ± 0.5 and Ωm to 0.315 ± 0.007. The redshift and distance-ladder desks use that rounded 67.4. This desk uses the table. The published age includes radiation. The integral on this page does not. The same table’s r* = 144.43 ± 0.26 Mpc is the comoving sound horizon, not the distance to last scattering, and this page does not compute it.
  • Riess et al. 2022, ApJL 934, L7 (arXiv:2112.04510) — baseline SH0ES H0 = 73.04 ± 1.04 km s−1 Mpc−1. The button uses the central value. The paper does not publish an Ωm or ΩΛ for that button. The densities stay at the Planck 2018 central values.
  • Colless and Dunn 1996, ApJ 458, 435 (arXiv:astro-ph/9508070) — the main Coma cluster, centered on NGC 4874, mean cz = 6853 km s−1, σcz = 1082 km s−1. This desk sets z = cz/c with c = 299,792.458 km/s. The paper prints cz. The velocity distribution is non-Gaussian. The same mean is the Hubble-flow preset on the distance ladder.
  • SIMBAD — 3C 273 — adopted redshift 0.15756751 ± 0.0005, referenced to Koss et al. 2022, ApJS 261, 2. The same figure is on the redshift desk. The distances here are the integrals at that z.
  • Bunker et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.07256 — GN-z11 systemic redshift z = 10.6034 ± 0.0013 from emission lines other than Lyman-alpha. The paper’s Lyman-alpha centroid is offset by 555 ± 32 km/s. This button uses 10.6034 and does not add the offset. The same choice as the redshift desk.
  • NIST CODATA — speed of light in vacuum — c = 299,792,458 m/s exactly, so 299,792.458 km/s.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — the astronomical unit is 149,597,870,700 m. The parsec is (648000/π) AU. The gigayear uses that parsec and a Julian year of 365.25 days of 86400 seconds. The Gly column uses 3.2616 light-years per parsec, the rounding on the magnitude desk, and it is not that time conversion.

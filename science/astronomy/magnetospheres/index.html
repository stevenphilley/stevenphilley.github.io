Magnetospheres

The nose of a magnetosphere is where the solar wind’s push balances the planet’s magnetic pressure. This page uses Rmp/R ≈ (k · Beq² / (2 μ0 ρ v²))1/6. k = 2 multiplies B². It is the assumption for a dipole compressed by the solar wind, and it is not a measured factor. Beq is the dipole field strength on the NASA NSSDCA sheet, in that sheet’s model radius. ρ is the proton mass times a number density. The opening density at 1 AU is the NOAA glossary’s near 5 protons per cm³. The opening speed is NOAA’s about 400 km/s. The glossary’s near 375 km/s stays a different sentence. Density at the planet is the 1 AU value divided by the square of the J2000 semimajor axis. Speed is not scaled. Earth’s arithmetic is 9.070 Re. NOAA NCEI says about 10 Earth radii. NASA Science says about six to 10. They are not averaged. Venus has an induced magnetic field. Mars has crustal remnant fields and no global dipole. The formula is not run for either one.

Simplified pressure-balance model, not mission-grade. Not a forecast, not a Kp product, and not an ephemeris. The bow shock curve is drawn outside the nose so the two lines can be told apart. The gap is not a measured magnetosheath. The aurora desk is where NOAA’s about 2 Earth radii of sheath is stacked on a different dayside span. The tail is cut off and is not to scale. Colors are a key, not photographs. A dash means the sheet does not print the line.

What the balance is

Magnetic pressure stands off the wind. The dynamic pressure is ρ v². The magnetic pressure is B² / (2 μ0). At the nose, a dipole would be Beq (R / Rmp)³. This page multiplies that magnetic pressure by k = 2, which is the same as writing k on B² inside the sixth root. Chapman and Ferraro’s 1930 pressure balance is the idea Bagenal 2024 names for this standoff. The k = 2 used here is this page’s factor. It is not a number printed on the fact sheets.

k = 2 multiplies B². A factor of 2 on the field itself would multiply B² by 4, and that would be a different formula. This page uses 2 on B². It does not also print a second standoff. Earth’s opening arithmetic is 9.070 model radii. That is the number set beside NOAA’s about 10 and NASA’s about six to 10.

The 1 AU wind is scaled in density only. The glossary says that at 1 AU velocities are near 375 km/s and proton and electron densities are near 5 per cm³. NOAA’s solar-wind page says the most common slow wind in the equatorial plane is about 400 km/s. Marshall says about 400 km/s, low at 300 km/s over streamers and high at 800 km/s over coronal holes. The slider runs from 300 to 800 so those marks sit on it. The opening speed is 400. The 375 button uses the glossary and does not average it with 400. SPARTAN’s about 3 to 10 particles per cm³ contains the 5. The 5 is the glossary’s sentence, not a middle of 3 to 10. Electron mass is not added to ρ. The proton mass is the CODATA 2022 value, 1.67262192595 × 10−27 kg.

The model radius is not the equatorial radius. Each sheet defines the R in “gauss-R³”. Earth’s is 6,378 km. The equatorial radius on the same sheet is 6378.137 km. Jupiter’s model radius is 71,398 km. The 1-bar equatorial radius is 71,492 km. The arithmetic is in the model radius. The other printing stays in the table. They are not averaged, and this page does not convert one into the other.

Saturn, the ice giants, Io, Ganymede

Saturn’s tilt line is 0.0. The Saturn sheet, model GSFC-Z3, prints dipole tilt to the rotational axis 0.0 and offset 0.038 Rs. The same sheet says the field has significant quadrapole and octupole moments, making approximation of the central field as an offset dipole difficult. The spelling “quadrapole” is the sheet’s. A GSFC planetary-magnetism page says Saturn’s magnetic axis seems to be exactly lined up with its rotation axis, within the accuracy of observations. The 0.0 and that sentence are not averaged. The Cassini magnetometer report’s two preferred states, near about 22 and about 27 Saturn radii, stay beside this page’s 17.100 Rs.

Uranus and Neptune are tilted and offset. Uranus, model GSFC-O3, prints tilt 58.6°, longitude of tilt 53.6°, and offset 0.352 Ru. Neptune, model GSFC-O8, prints tilt 46.9°, longitude 288° in the IAU convention, and offset 0.485 Rn. The GSFC page says the magnetic axes of Uranus and Neptune are inclined by about 60° to their rotation axes. 58.6 and 46.9 are the sheet numbers. About 60° is the other page. 46.9 is not about 60. They are not averaged. Paty et al. 2020 put the subsolar magnetopause at 18 RU and 23 to 26 RN. This arithmetic is 22.016 Ru and 21.837 Rn.

Io feeds a torus. Ganymede has its own field. Bagenal 2024 says Io ejects about one ton per second of gases from its sulfur dioxide atmosphere, dissociated and ionized into a torus of electrons and sulfur and oxygen ions. The same paper later says over a ton per second. The two wordings are not averaged. NASA Science calls Io the most volcanically active world in the solar system. It does not print the ton-per-second line, and this page does not add a torus radius that page does not print. NASA Science says Ganymede is the only moon known to have its own magnetic field, and that a metallic iron core generates it. Bagenal 2024 says Ganymede is the only moon known to have its own internal magnetic dynamo, producing a magnetosphere within a magnetosphere. This page does not compute Ganymede’s standoff. The dipole in the button is Jupiter’s.

A dynamo is moving conductor. NASA Science says Earth’s magnetosphere is generated by the convective motion of charged, molten iron in the outer core. Ganymede’s page names a metallic iron core. The GSFC page says what produces Jupiter’s field is still unclear, and that one widely held theory is hydrogen compressed until it conducts. The same page says the origin of the giant-planet fields is unknown, that Saturn is big enough to produce metallic hydrogen in its core, and that Uranus and Neptune are not. Those are that page’s sentences. This desk does not turn them into a recipe, and it does not replace the printed dipole strengths.

Venus and Mars

Venus has an induced magnetic field. NASA Science says Venus does not have its own internally generated magnetic field. It has an induced magnetic field, made where the Sun’s magnetic field meets the outer atmosphere. Ultraviolet light excites gases into an ionosphere, and the solar wind carries the Sun’s field into that ionosphere. The induced field envelops the planet and is shaped like a teardrop, or the tail of a comet. The Venus fact sheet has no magnetosphere block. This page does not invent a dipole strength for it. The temperature and pressure sentences on that Venus page are not copied here. The fact-sheet temperatures live on the temperatures desk.

Mars has remnant crustal fields and no global dipole. NASA Basics of Space Flight prints a global-magnetic-field line of None for Venus and for Mars, Faint for Mercury, and Yes for Earth. The same chapter says that among the terrestrial planets only Earth has a substantial planetary magnetic field, and that Mars and the Moon have localized regional magnetic fields and no global field. The fact-sheet notes say Mars and the Moon have localized regional magnetic fields but no global field. NASA’s MAVEN release says Mars lost its global magnetic field and now has remnant fossil magnetic fields in regions of the crust, and it calls Venus a planet with no magnetic field of its own. The Mars fact sheet has no magnetosphere block. The formula is not run.

What the words mean

Magnetopause
The sunward boundary in this drawing, where the formula places the nose. Distance is in the sheet’s model radii. The flanks and the tail are a sketch. The tail is cut off.
Bow shock
A dashed curve outside the nose. It is drawn farther out so the two curves separate. It is not NOAA’s about 2 Earth radii, and it is not a measured shock. The aurora desk stacks that 2 on a different dayside span.
Dipole field strength
The fact sheet’s gauss-R³ line. For a centered dipole it is the equatorial field at one model radius. This page uses it as Beq. The surface range on the next line is the field over the surface, and it is not substituted.
k = 2
The factor on B² in the formula. It stands for a dipole compressed by the solar wind. It is an assumption of this page. It is not a magnetometer product, and it is not a doubling of the field.
Dynamic pressure
ρ v², with ρ equal to the local proton number density times 1.67262192595 × 10−27 kg. Printed in nanopascals to four decimals. Winslow et al. 2013’s 14.3 nPa is a different pressure, kept beside Mercury’s arithmetic.
What the sixth root leaves out
Plasma pressure inside the magnetosphere, which Bagenal 2024 says inflates Jupiter from a dipolar stand-off of about 42 RJ to over 90 RJ. An offset dipole, which the Saturn sheet says is a difficult approximation. Induction, reconnection, and a real bow shock. The solar cycle, which changes the wind and is the solar cycle desk. The ejections, which are the solar wind desk. The trapped belts, which are the Van Allen desk. Not mission-grade.

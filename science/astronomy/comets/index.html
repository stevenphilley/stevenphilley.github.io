Vol. Science · Astronomy · The tail

How a comet
grows a tail

A comet is a small icy body on a long ellipse around the Sun. The frozen part is the nucleus. Near the Sun it grows a coma, and then two tails. The ion tail is blown straight away from the Sun. The dust tail is heavier, so it curves and lags along the orbit. This page draws that ellipse from a perihelion distance q and an eccentricity e, or from the semi-major axis a and e. a = q / (1 − e). The comet’s place comes from Kepler’s equation, so it crawls near aphelion and rushes past perihelion. Speed is vis-viva. The period is the two-body period from the Sun Fact Sheet GM. Four buttons load osculating elements from the JPL Small-Body Database. A dashed circle is 3 AU, the distance past which Cochran, McKay, and Moulane 2025 say water ice stays frozen. An eccentricity above 1 does not close. That hyperbola, and 1I, 2I, and 3I, are the interstellar desk.

Nucleus, coma, and the two tails

Nucleus
The frozen body. NASA Science calls it icy chunks and frozen gases with bits of embedded dust, often a few miles across, and the leftover of the early solar system. Far from the Sun it stays cold. This drawing is a point, not a measured nucleus.
Coma
The atmosphere that appears as the nucleus warms. NASA Science says the Sun’s heat turns ices to gas and the coma may extend hundreds of thousands of kilometers. NASA’s Basics of Space Flight says that, within a few AU, the volatiles evaporate and carry dust with them. The glow around the head on this page stands for that coma. It is not a measured diameter.
Ion tail
Gas that has been ionized, pushed by the solar wind. NASA’s Basics of Space Flight says the ion tail is accelerated so strongly that it appears as a nearly straight line extending away from the comet, opposite the Sun. This page draws that line radially anti-sunward. A real ion tail can kink when the solar wind changes. That kink is not drawn.
Dust tail
Solid grains, pushed by sunlight more gently than the ions. The same NASA chapter says relatively massive dust tails are accelerated slowly and tend to be curved. Grains released earlier lag along the orbit. This page samples those earlier places and pushes them outward. The curve is a schematic, not a trajectory of a grain.
Short and long period
NASA Science calls comets that take less than 200 years short-period, and traces that population to the Kuiper belt. The dwarf planets in that region, and the classical band from about 40 to 50 AU, are the dwarf planets desk. Long-period comets, on that page, arrive from the Oort cloud and can take as long as 30 million years. JPL’s classes cut more finely: Halley-type is the classical 20 years < P < 200 years, Jupiter-family is Levison and Duncan’s 2 < TJ < 3, and Encke-type is TJ > 3 with a semi-major axis inside Jupiter’s. Hale–Bopp’s printed period is thousands of years, so it is long-period on NASA’s 200-year line.
The 3 AU line
Cochran, McKay, and Moulane 2025 write that H2O stays in its ice form farther from the Sun than 3.0 AU, and that most of a nucleus’s activity should occur within about 3.0 AU. They also report CN from C/2017 K2 at 3.39 AU, so the line is not a switch every comet obeys. NASA’s Basics of Space Flight says the surface begins to warm within a few AU, without quoting 3. More volatile ices, such as CO and CO2, can drive activity farther out. The card says “commonly sublimating” inside 3 AU and “generally still frozen” outside it. It does not compute a production rate.

  • NASA NSSDCA — Earth Fact Sheet — sidereal orbit period 365.256 days. That day count is the year when this page also prints the two-body period in days. It is the same unit as the Kepler desk.
  • NASA Science — Comet facts — a nucleus of icy chunks, frozen gases, and dust; a coma that may extend hundreds of thousands of kilometers; sunlight and the solar wind blow dust and gas away from the Sun into a dust tail and an ion tail. Long-period comets arrive from the Oort cloud, given there as about 100,000 AU, and can take as long as 30 million years.
  • NASA — Basics of Space Flight, comets — within a few AU the volatiles evaporate and carry dust, forming the coma. Dust tails curve because the grains are accelerated slowly. The ion tail is a nearly straight line opposite the Sun. The Oort cloud is described there as about 50,000 AU. The Kuiper belt, about 30 to 100 AU, is called the source of the short-period comets. Those two NASA pages do not quote the same Oort distance. This page does not pick one.
  • Cochran, McKay, and Moulane 2025, Planet. Sci. J. 6, 272 — “H2O stays in its ice form when further from the Sun than 3.0 au. The card uses their 3.0 AU sentence. It does not copy a production rate.
  • Vis-viva desk — the same formula, v = √[μ (2/r − 1/a)], with the same Sun GM. Earth’s perihelion there rounds to the fact sheet’s 30.29 km/s.
  • Eccentricity desk — rp = a(1−e) and ra = a(1+e). That page’s Halley button is the comet fact sheet’s rounded 17.94 AU and 0.967, not these JPL elements.
  • Meteors desk — the Eta Aquariids and the Orionids list 1P/Halley as the parent body, from JPL’s meteor-stream table. This page does not draw a meteor stream.

Solar system
map

A clickable schematic of our neighborhood — Sun through Neptune, with Pluto labeled as a dwarf planet. Set a day and local time (defaults to now) and the planets move to approximate positions for that moment. Play walks the date forward; set the speed (0.5×–10×) to go slower or faster. Also see the Moon phases dial, why eclipses are rare (the Moon’s orbit is tilted about 5°, so most new and full Moons miss), and why the year has seasons (axial tilt, not distance), then continue with the local sky (city or ZIP, default Campbell), a stars types & lives color lesson, nearest stars, exoplanets, and a Milky Way starter. Light itself — radio through gamma — has a literacy desk at EM spectrum. The force that schedules this map has its own literacy desk: Gravity. Part of the science desk; also see Earth’s atmosphere.

Educational schematic with compressed orbital scale and exaggerated planet sizes so everything fits and stays visible. Planet angles are approximate mean heliocentric longitudes for the chosen date and time (circular orbits, NASA/JPL-style mean elements) — not a precision ephemeris. Distances and periods are mean values from public NASA fact sheets.

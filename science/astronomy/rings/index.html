Vol. Science · Astronomy · Ring systems

Four planets
wear a ring

A ring is material in orbit around a planet. Saturn, Jupiter, Uranus, and Neptune have them. Distances on this page are kilometers from the planet’s center, from the NASA NSSDCA ring fact sheets. An edge the sheet does not print is a dash. The fluid Roche distance is d = 2.44 R (ρplanet / ρ)1/3. The rigid distance uses 1.26. R is the volumetric mean radius, the same R as the Roche desk. Equal density, ratio 1, is the ring comparison. For Saturn that is 142,086 km and 73,372 km. Rhea’s density, 1,240 kg/m³, is the Roche desk’s pair: 116,698 km and 60,262 km. The Mimas 2:1 is computed here from Mimas’s semimajor axis and Saturn’s GM. It is 116,870 km. The resonances desk is that kind of period ratio. The Hill sphere is a different distance. Enceladus, which feeds the E ring, is on the ocean worlds desk.

Saturn’s volumetric mean radius is 58,232 km and its mean density is 687 kg/m³. The fluid distance is 2.44 × R × (ratio)^(1/3). The rigid distance uses 1.26. Equal density, ratio 1, puts them at 142,086 km and 73,372 km. Rhea’s 1,240 kg/m³, the Roche desk’s pair, puts them at 116,698 km and 60,262 km. The arcs use equal density. Mimas is at 185,520 km. Kepler with Saturn’s GM gives a period of 0.943529 days. The sheet prints 0.9424218 days. The 2:1 radius is 116,870 km. The Cassini division row has no radius. A dash is an edge the sheet does not print. Schematic, not an ephemeris.

Why the bright rings sit inward

Inside the fluid distance, loose material does not gather into a moon. That is the Roche desk’s reading of the same formula. NASA Science says Saturn’s rings are thought to be pieces of comets, asteroids, or shattered moons that broke up before they reached the planet, torn apart by Saturn’s gravity. On this page the equal-density fluid distance for Saturn is 142,086 km. The A ring’s outer edge, 136,780 km, is inside it. The E ring’s outer edge, 480,000 km, is outside it. The bright A, B, and C rings are the ones inside. The faint E ring is not.

The other three are not copies of Saturn. Jupiter’s equal-density fluid distance is 170,583 km. The Thebe extension runs to 280,000 km, outside that distance. The halo’s inner printed edge, 89,400 km, sits just outside the rigid distance, 88,088 km. NASA Science says Jupiter’s rings may be dust kicked up when meteoroids hit the small inner moons. Uranus’s Epsilon ring is printed at 51,149 km, inside the equal-density fluid distance of 61,883 km. R/2003 U 1 is printed at 97,700 km, outside it. Neptune’s Adams ring is printed at 62,933 km, outside the equal-density fluid distance of 60,078 km. Galle is printed at about 41,900 km, between the rigid distance, 31,024 km, and the fluid one.

Two radii, on purpose. The Roche formula uses the volumetric mean. Saturn’s is 58,232 km. The rings sheet’s “Saturn radii” column divides by the equatorial radius, 60,268 km. The A outer edge is 136,780 km, which that column prints as 2.270 equatorial radii. Divided by 58,232 km it is 2.349 volumetric radii. The Roche desk already prints that 2.349. This page does not mix the two divisors.

The Cassini Division and Mimas

The sheet does not print a Cassini radius. The Cassini division row has optical depth, albedo, thickness, and surface density. The radius cell is empty. The row sits between the B outer edge, 117,507 km, and the A inner edge, 122,340 km. Those are the neighboring edges. They are not labeled as the division’s edges on the sheet. NASA Science calls the Cassini Division a gap 2,920 miles (4,700 kilometers) wide that separates the A and B rings. A Cassini caption calls the same division 2,980 miles (4,800 kilometers) wide. The two widths are not averaged.

The 2:1 is computed here. An interior 2:1 means the ring particle finishes 2 orbits while Mimas finishes 1. That is the p:q wording on the resonances desk. Mimas’s semimajor axis is 185.52 × 10³ km, so 185,520 km. Saturn’s GM is 37.931 × 106 km³/s². Kepler’s third law is P = 2π √(a³ / GM). That period is 0.943529 days. The satellite sheet prints 0.9424218 days. This page does not replace one with the other. The 2:1 period is the computed period halved. The radius is (GM × P2:1² / (4π²))1/3, which is 116,870 km. The same number is 185,520 × (1/2)2/3, because the GM cancels. 116,870 km is 637 km inside the B outer edge. The caption says particles in the division orbit Saturn almost exactly twice for every orbit of Mimas, and that the nudges sculpt the outer edge of the B ring. This line is the Keplerian 2:1. It is not moved onto that edge.

Shepherds

Prometheus and Pandora, at the F ring. The rings sheet lists the F ring at 139,826 km and says rings narrower than 1,000 km are listed by inner edge. No outer edge is printed. Prometheus’s semimajor axis is 139.353 × 10³ km, which is 139,353 km. Pandora’s is 141.700 × 10³ km, which is 141,700 km. NASA Science says Prometheus acts as a shepherding moon, constraining the inner edge of the F ring, and gives the orbit as 87,000 miles (139,000 kilometers). Pandora’s page says Pandora tends to disrupt the F ring while Prometheus helps keep the primary ring in place, and gives 88,000 miles (142,000 kilometers). The marks use the fact-sheet axes. The mile sentences are the rounded lines. They are not averaged.

Cordelia and Ophelia, at Epsilon. The Uranus sheet prints Epsilon at 51,149 km, with a width of 20–96 km, and does not print an inner edge or an outer edge. Cordelia is at 49.80 × 10³ km. Ophelia is at 53.80 × 10³ km. NASA Science says Cordelia and Ophelia are shepherd moons whose gravity keeps the particles of the Epsilon ring from dispersing. The sheet leaves both densities blank. No density is invented.

Galatea, just inward of Adams. Galatea’s semimajor axis is 61.953 × 10³ km. The rings sheet prints an unnamed ring at 61,953 km and the Adams ring at 62,933 km. NASA Science says the gravitational effects of Galatea, a moon just inward from the ring, stabilize the Adams arcs. Galatea’s page says the moon’s gravity is believed to cause disturbances in the ring system. The density cell is blank.

Metis, Adrastea, Amalthea, and Thebe. The axes are 128.0, 129.0, 181.4, and 221.9 × 10³ km. NASA Science says Metis and Adrastea orbit inside the main ring and are a source of its material, and that Amalthea and Thebe provide the material for the gossamer ring. The rings sheet’s main ring ends at 128,940 km. Adrastea’s 129,000 km is outside that edge. Both sentences stay. Amalthea’s sheet axis, 181,400 km, and the Amalthea-ring outer edge, 181,350 km, are 50 km apart. Thebe’s 221,900 km is the number the ring sheet prints as the Thebe ring’s outer edge and the extension’s inner edge. Only Amalthea has a density on the satellite sheet, 3,100 kg/m³. The slider can use it. It is the moon’s density, not a ring-particle density.

Thickness and age

The sheet does not have one thickness. Saturn’s rings sheet prints thickness in meters: the C ring 5, the B ring 5–10, the Cassini division 20, the A ring 10–30, the G ring 105, and the E ring 107. NASA Science says the vertical height is typically about 30 feet (10 meters) in the main rings. The Cassini rings page says the rings are generally about 30 feet (10 meters) thick or so. This drawing does not pick a thickness, and it does not draw one.

This desk does not pick an age. A Cassini mass page says the findings indicate that Saturn’s rings formed between 10 million and 100 million years ago. An Ames page says the rings’ age has been hard to pin down, and that the rings could not have been exposed to the micrometeoroid hail for more than a few hundred million years. Those are two NASA sentences. They are not averaged into one age. NASA Science says Neptune’s rings are thought to be relatively young and short-lived, and it does not give a year count in that sentence. None is added here.

What the words mean

Roche, fluid and rigid
d = 2.44 R (ρplanet / ρ)1/3, and the same cube root with 1.26. R is the volumetric mean radius. Equal density sets the ratio to 1. Rhea’s 1,240 kg/m³ is the Saturn pair on the Roche desk. The arcs follow the button. The rings do not.
A dash
The fact sheet did not print that edge. A width is not turned into an inner edge and an outer edge. A tilde stays a tilde. Two NASA printings of the same ring are not averaged.
2:1
The inner body finishes two orbits while Mimas finishes one. The radius uses Mimas’s 185,520 km and Saturn’s GM. The sheet’s 0.9424218 days is cited and not used. The result is 116,870 km, 637 km inside the B outer edge.
Shepherd
A moon whose gravity is cited as holding or disturbing a ring. Prometheus, Cordelia, and Ophelia are named that way on NASA Science pages. Pandora is cited as disrupting the F ring. Galatea is cited as stabilizing the Adams arcs. The word is not added where the page does not use it.
What the circles leave out
Eccentricity, inclination, ring thickness, spokes, propellers, and every gap narrower than the named rings. Orbits are circles about the planet. The planet disk is the volumetric radius, not a photograph. Close and Full sheet are two scales. Mean densities. Not an ephemeris and not a mission tool.

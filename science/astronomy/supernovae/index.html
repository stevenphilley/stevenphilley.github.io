Vol. Science · Astronomy · The decay

Nickel, cobalt,
then iron

The blast spends itself. What keeps a supernova bright afterwards is a decay chain. 56Ni captures an electron and becomes 56Co. 56Co becomes stable 56Fe, mostly by electron capture, and in about 19 decays out of 100 by emitting a positron. Nadyozhin 1994 prints the half-lives as 6.10 days and 77.12 days, and the mean lives in his energy formula as 8.80 days and 111.3 days. The energy that can heat the gas, with neutrinos left out, is 1.75 MeV per nickel decay and 3.73 MeV per cobalt decay. This page deposits all of that. That is the full gamma-ray trapping assumption. A Type Ia is the bright candle on the distance ladder. The remnant, if one is left, is the compact-star desk. The rough path that ends in the explosion is stellar lifetime.

Educational calculator. Not a radiative-transfer model, not a light-curve fit, and not a catalog. The power is the two-step Bateman solution for 56Ni → 56Co → 56Fe. Half-lives 6.10 d and 77.12 d, mean lives 8.80 d and 111.3 d, and deposited energies 1.75 MeV and 3.73 MeV are Nadyozhin 1994. Full gamma-ray trapping: gamma rays and positron kinetic energy are deposited, and neutrinos are not. Diffusion is not computed. The curve falls from day 0. The observed peak is shaped by diffusion. The fiducial is 0.6 solar masses of 56Ni. Timmes, Brown, and Truran 2003 say the average peak B and V magnitudes of nearby Type Ia supernovae strongly imply that a fiducial event produces ≈ 0.6 M☉. The ≈ is theirs. They cite Saha et al. 1999 and Gibson et al. 2000. This page has not re-read those two. This is not the nickel mass of one named supernova.

Day 24.25. Nadyozhin’s cobalt-number maximum, from his 8.80 d and 111.3 d, recomputes to 24.2469 days, which rounds to the 24.25 days he prints. That day is when the cobalt count is highest. It is not the optical peak. Full trapping. Neutrinos stay out: 0.41 MeV per nickel decay and 0.84 MeV per cobalt decay. Deposited energy is 1.75 MeV and 3.73 MeV. By day 800 of SN 1987A, Suntzeff and Bouchet say more than half the bolometric flux was escaping as high-energy photons. This curve does not remove that leak.

Standard candle

The mean peak is a magnitude. This page is a power.

Type Ia supernovae are the bright rung on the distance ladder. That desk holds the mean peak at MB = −19.25. Richardson, Jenkins, Wright, and Maddox 2014, Table 1, bias-corrected normal supernovae: the uncertainty on the mean is ±0.20, the sample is 239, and the width of the distribution is 0.50 mag. One event is not known to 0.20 mag. No decline-rate correction is applied there, and none is applied here.

Hillebrandt and Niemeyer 2000 quote MB ≈ MV ≈ −19.30 ± 0.03 + 5 log(H0/60), with a dispersion of 0.3 mag or less. That −19.30 is the value at H0 = 60. It moves when H0 moves. It is not the candle, and this page does not replace −19.25 with it. The nickel mass sets the radioactive power. It does not, by itself, set the absolute magnitude.

Literacy · what the curve is

Bateman solution
NNi = N0 exp(−t/τNi). NCo starts at zero and is N0 [τCo / (τCo − τNi)] [exp(−t/τCo) − exp(−t/τNi)]. The power is the number divided by the mean life, times the deposited energy of that decay. τNi = 8.80 d and τCo = 111.3 d are the mean lives Nadyozhin prints. 6.10 / ln 2 rounds to 8.80. 77.12 / ln 2 is 111.26, which he rounds to 111.3. The calculator uses 8.80 and 111.3.
Deposited energy
Per 56Ni decay, 1.75 MeV in gamma rays. Per 56Co decay, 3.61 MeV in gamma rays plus 0.12 MeV of positron kinetic energy, 3.73 MeV. Neutrinos are 0.41 MeV and 0.84 MeV. They are not in the power. The gamma total for cobalt includes the annihilation photons he counts, 38 per 100 decays.
Full trapping
Every gamma ray, and the positron’s kinetic energy, is counted as heat in the ejecta. Real ejecta become thin to gamma rays. For SN 1987A, Suntzeff and Bouchet say that by day 800 more than half the bolometric flux was escaping as high-energy photons. This page does not compute that leak.
Arnett’s rule
Seitenzahl and Townsley state it this way, citing Arnett 1982: the bolometric luminosity of a Type Ia at maximum light is proportional, with a factor near one, to the instantaneous energy deposition rate. The factor depends on explosion energy, opacity, ejecta mass, and where the nickel sits. This page does not evaluate it and does not mark a peak day. The curve here is highest at day 0 because diffusion is left out. The optical light rises while photons are still trapped, then falls.
Cobalt maximum
The cobalt count, and so the cobalt power, peaks at τNi τCo / (τCo − τNi) × ln(τCo/τNi). From 8.80 and 111.3 that is 24.2469 days, which rounds to the 24.25 days Nadyozhin prints. The button uses 24.25. It is not the optical peak.
Two tails
A Type Ia has no hydrogen plateau. Its light is this chain from early on, reprocessed by diffusion. A core-collapse event such as SN 1987A has a broad maximum while a recombination front moves through the hydrogen envelope, and the same chain powers the later decline. Suntzeff and Bouchet describe that for 1987A. This page draws only the decay power.
The arithmetic
One nucleus is 56 u. u is the 2022 CODATA atomic mass constant, 1.66053906892×10⁻²⁷ kg. One MeV is 10⁶ × e joules, and e is the exact elementary charge. A solar mass is the IAU 2015 nominal GM☉, 1.3271244×10²⁰, divided by the 2022 CODATA G, 6.67430×10⁻¹¹, which is 1.9884098707 × 10³⁰ kg. The count per solar mass is 2.13830 × 10⁵⁵. The combined coefficients recompute to 6.44240 × 10⁴³ and 1.44295 × 10⁴³ erg s⁻¹ per solar mass. Nadyozhin prints 6.45 × 10⁴³ and 1.45 × 10⁴³. They are not averaged. The curves use the Bateman solution. L☉ is the IAU nominal 3.828×10²⁶ W, for the sun count only.

  • Arnett and Fu 1989, ApJ 340, 396 — 0.073 ± 0.015 M☉ of freshly synthesized 56Ni to fit the bolometric light curve. The uncertainty is dominated by the distance. Shown beside 0.071. Not averaged with it.
  • Richardson, Jenkins, Wright, and Maddox 2014, AJ 147, 118 — Table 1, bias-corrected normal Type Ia, MB = −19.25 ± 0.20. The candle on the distance ladder. This page does not recompute it.
  • Hillebrandt and Niemeyer 2000, ARA&A 38, 191 — MB ≈ MV ≈ −19.30 ± 0.03 + 5 log(H0/60). The −19.30 belongs to H0 = 60. It is not the candle.

  • NIST — Newtonian constant of gravitation — 2022 CODATA G = 6.67430×10⁻¹¹. Used only to turn the nominal GM☉ into a mass for the nucleus count.

Tidal locking

A tidal torque despins a body toward synchronous rotation: one turn per orbit, one face toward its partner. The partner raises two bulges. If the body spins faster than it orbits, those bulges are carried ahead of the partner. The partner pulls back on the near bulge, and the spin slows. When the spin matches the orbit, the bulge stays on the line of centers and that misalignment torque stops. The order-of-magnitude time used here is tlock = (ω a6 I Q) / (3 G mp2 k2 R5), with I = f ms R2. It is the inversion of equation (2) in Hinkel & Kane 2013. For Moon–Earth, with the fact-sheet mass, radius, I/MR2 = 0.394, and distance, and with the assumed initial spin of 13.5 hours, Q = 100, and k2 = 0.3, the arithmetic is 3.40464 million years. Order of magnitude only. Q and k2 are very uncertain. The ocean picture of two bulges is the tides desk. The peek past a locked lunar face is libration. A period ratio of two orbits is resonances. Whether a close M-dwarf planet sits in the insolation strip is the habitable zone.

Educational approximation, not a tidal-evolution model. The torque is taken at the initial spin and held at that magnitude. The orbital rate is not subtracted, the semimajor axis is held fixed, and eccentricity is left out. Real k2/Q depends on frequency. Cuartas-Restrepo et al., arXiv:1606.07546, equation (5), print twice this time and also cite Gladman et al. 1996. The factor of two is not substituted. Hinkel & Kane attribute their equation (2) to Peale 1977 and Gladman, Quinn, Nicholson, and Rand, Icarus 122, 166 (1996). This page has not re-read those two. What the number is

A misaligned bulge, and a torque. NASA Science describes the Moon’s case. Earth’s gravity raised a bulge. The bulge stayed a little out of alignment, because the rock took time to rise and fall, and gravity kept pulling that bulge toward alignment. Flexing turned spin energy into heat, and the spin slowed until one turn took as long as one orbit. In that state the bulge no longer shifted relative to Earth. The three buttons are that story, drawn large. “Spins faster” puts the bulge ahead of the partner, so the torque slows the spin. “Spins slower” puts the bulge behind, so the torque speeds the spin up. “Synchronous” puts the bulge on the line of centers. The angle is not a measurement.

The one-third form, and the factor of two beside it. Hinkel & Kane 2013, equation (2), define a tidal locking radius rTL ≈ (3 G Mp2 k2 R5 tL / (ω0 I Q))1/6. Solving for tL is this page’s formula. They set k2 = 0.3 and I ≈ 0.4 M R2, and, citing Kasting et al. 1993, one rotation per 13.5 hours and Q = 100. They attribute the expression to Peale 1977 and Gladman et al. 1996. Cuartas-Restrepo et al., arXiv:1606.07546, equation (5), write tlock = 2 Ω0 C a6 Q / (3 G M2 R5 k2) and also cite Gladman. That is twice this page’s time. This page uses the one-third form. The two are not averaged. A factor of two sits inside the uncertainty of Q and k2. This page has not re-read Gladman or Peale.

Today’s distance is not the distance at locking. NASA Science says the Moon formed closer and is still drifting away, about an inch and a half (4 cm) a year, while the fact sheet prints a recession rate of 3.8 cm/yr. Those two rates are not averaged. Because tlock scales as a6, a body that locked closer in did so in a much shorter time than the number at today’s semimajor axis. Half the distance divides the time by 64. The chart is that power. The formula also ignores that the orbit itself changes while the spin changes.

Spin–orbit resonances, and a mutual lock. Synchronous rotation is the 1:1 resonance. Mercury is not in it. The Mercury sheet’s 1407.6 hours and 87.969 days are 1.4999 orbits per sidereal rotation, near 3/2, not exactly 3/2. NASA Science says Mercury rotates three times for every two revolutions, and calls that a resonance. The time on the Mercury button is a despinning time toward 1:1. It is not a capture time into the 3:2. The planet-days desk keeps the same two periods beside the solar day. Pluto and Charon are mutually locked. The Pluto sheet’s sidereal rotation is −153.2928 hours. Dividing the absolute value by 24 gives 6.3872 days, the same figure the Charon block prints for both the orbit and the rotation. NASA Science calls that mutual tidal locking: the same faces stay toward each other. This page does not run the formula for that pair. Each body torques the other, and the formula here holds the partner fixed. The separation is the barycenter desk.

What the words mean

Synchronous rotation
One spin per orbit, so the same face stays toward the partner. NASA Science calls the Moon’s case synchronous tidal locking. The Moon Fact Sheet’s 655.720 hours and 27.3217 days are that end state. The libration desk is why the face still peeks past the limb.
Q and k2
Q is the dissipation function. A smaller Q means more of the tidal flexing turns into heat, and a shorter time. k2 is the second-order Love number, a measure of how much the gravity field yields. Hinkel & Kane use k2 = 0.3 and, citing Kasting et al. 1993, Q = 100. Both are assumptions here. They are not measurements of the Moon, Io, Mercury, or Proxima b.
Initial spin
ω in the formula is the spin the body is despun from, in radians per second. 13.5 hours is the figure Hinkel & Kane take from Kasting et al. 1993. It is not the Moon’s 655.720 hours, not Io’s synchronous period, and not Mercury’s 1407.6 hours. Those are present states.
a^6
The time grows with the sixth power of distance and falls with the square of the partner’s mass and the fifth power of the satellite’s radius. The log-log line has slope 6 when everything except distance is fixed. That is why a habitable-zone planet close to an M dwarf can lock quickly, and why the same formula at Mercury’s distance is hundreds of millions of years. A place in the habitable zone is not a verdict that the planet is locked, and a short time is not a verdict that it is habitable.
3:2
Mercury’s spin–orbit resonance. Three sidereal rotations in two sidereal orbits, near the fact-sheet ratio 1.4999, not exactly 3/2. NASA Science names it a 3:2 resonance and sets it beside a planet that might not be in 1:1. The resonances desk is a ratio of two orbits, not a spin and one orbit.
What this picture leaves out
Permanent triaxial torque, capture probability, obliquity, and the back-reaction on the orbit. Also the frequency dependence of k2/Q, which can change the time by more than the factor of two between the two cited formulas. Io’s sheet eccentricity is 0.004 and Mercury’s is 0.2056. Neither enters the arithmetic. The Laplace chain that keeps Io eccentric is the resonances desk. Earth’s ocean bulges, which lead the Moon today and slow Earth’s spin, are the tides desk.

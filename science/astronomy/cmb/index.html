Cosmic microwave
background

The cosmic microwave background is the light left over from when the universe became transparent. It is the same glow in every direction, cooled by the expansion since last scattering. Fixsen 2009 combines the measurements then in the literature and gives the temperature as 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K. At that temperature the Planck spectrum on this page, built from the exact CODATA 2018 values of h, c, and k, peaks at 1.0632 mm and at 160.229 GHz. Both sit in the microwave band of the NASA Imagine classroom chart. The photon density is 410.7 per cubic centimeter. The energy density aT⁴ is 4.1747 × 10⁻¹⁴ J/m³. The radiation constant computed here is 7.5657 × 10⁻¹⁶ J m⁻³ K⁻⁴.

A perfect blackbody is Planck’s law and nothing else. This desk evaluates that law. It does not refit the FIRAS spectrum Fixsen recalibrates. The wavelength peak uses b = hc/(kx), with x the root of (5 − x)eˣ = 5. That b prints as 2.897771955 × 10⁻³ m·K, the CODATA digits. The frequency peak is a different root, (3 − x)eˣ = 3, which is 58.789 GHz per kelvin. c/λmax today is 281.968 GHz. That is not the peak of Bν. The blackbody desk plots Bλ for stars. This desk plots Bν.

Expansion stretches the wavelengths, so an ideal blackbody that was hotter scales as T(z) = T0(1+z). That is the redshift slider. It is not a measured spectrum at that redshift. At z = 1 the temperature is 5.45096 K. At z = 6, a round redshift in the era of early galaxies and not one catalog object, it is 19.07836 K. Planck 2018 results VI, Table 2, gives the redshift of last scattering as z* = 1089.92 ± 0.25. The temperature there is 2973.28 K, and the wavelength peak has moved to 974.6 nm, infrared, next to the visible window’s 700 nm edge. The frequency peak is 174.797 THz, also infrared.

Motion of the Solar System through this light produces a dipole, ΔT = T0 v/c. Planck 2018 results I, Table 2, lists the amplitude as 3362.08 ± 0.99 µK. Divided by Fixsen’s T0 and multiplied by c, that amplitude is 369.82 km/s, and ΔT/T is 1.2336 × 10⁻³. The smaller hot and cold spots, once that dipole is removed, are about 1 part in 10⁵. This page draws the dipole and not those spots. Ages and distances at the same z are the cosmology desk. The shift of a spectral line is the redshift desk.

Why the glow looks like this

What it is
The cosmic microwave background is thermal light from the early universe, released when the universe became transparent and stretched ever since. Fixsen 2009 is the temperature used here, 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K. This page does not recompute that average. It starts from it.
Planck’s law, per frequency
Bν = 2hν³/c² / (ehν/kT − 1). The curve is that function divided by its peak. h, c, and k are the exact constants. The blackbody desk uses the wavelength form, which peaks somewhere else.
Two peaks
λmax T = b, with b from the root of (5 − x)eˣ = 5. The peak of Bν is ν/T from the root of (3 − x)eˣ = 3. At 2.72548 K those are 1.0632 mm and 160.229 GHz. c/λmax is 281.968 GHz.
Photons and energy
n = 16π ζ(3) (kT/hc)³. ζ(3) is the sum of 1/n³, computed in the page. At T0 that is 410.7 per cm³, about 411. The energy density is aT⁴, with a = 4σ/c and σ = 2π⁵k⁴/(15h³c²). a prints as 7.5657 × 10⁻¹⁶ J m⁻³ K⁻⁴.
Redshift
Ideal blackbody scaling: T(z) = T0(1+z). The slider is that product. It is not a spectrum measured at that redshift, and it leaves out foregrounds. z = 6 is a round teaching redshift, not a named galaxy. The line shift itself is the redshift desk.
Last scattering
Planck 2018 results VI, Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing: z* = 1089.92 ± 0.25. This preset uses the central value. T0(1+z*) is 2973.28 K. The wavelength peak is then 974.6 nm, infrared on the NASA Imagine chart, whose visible window is about 400–700 nm. The cosmology desk uses the same z* for a distance, not for this temperature.
Dipole and the small spots
ΔT = T0 v/c is the amplitude of the hot and cold poles, so the poles differ by twice that. Planck 2018 results I, Table 2, gives 3362.08 ± 0.99 µK in the direction (l, b) = (264.021 ± 0.011, 48.253 ± 0.005) degrees. This page uses the amplitude. It does not draw that direction. With Fixsen’s T0 the speed is 369.82 km/s and ΔT/T is 1.2336 × 10⁻³. The primordial anisotropies, after the dipole is taken off, are about 1 part in 10⁵. They are not on this disk.

Sources

  • Fixsen 2009, ApJ 707, 916 — the temperature of the cosmic microwave background. The determination from the measurements in that paper is 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K. This desk uses the central value. DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/707/2/916.
  • Planck Collaboration 2020, A&A 641, A1 (arXiv:1807.06205) — Planck 2018 results I. Table 2, Planck 2018 row, adopts the HFI amplitude 3362.08 ± 0.99 µK, toward galactic (l, b) = (264.021 ± 0.011, 48.253 ± 0.005) degrees. The speed on this page is c times that amplitude divided by Fixsen’s T0.
  • Planck Collaboration 2020, A&A 641, A6 (arXiv:1807.06209) — Planck 2018 results VI. Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing: z* = 1089.92 ± 0.25, the redshift of last scattering. The same central value is the CMB button on the cosmology desk. This page multiplies T0 by (1+z*) and does not integrate a distance.
  • Tiesinga, Mohr, Newell, and Taylor 2021, Rev. Mod. Phys. 93, 025010 — CODATA recommended values of the fundamental physical constants: 2018. h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K, and c = 299792458 m/s are exact. NIST’s value pages for h, k, c, and the Wien constant print the same exact inputs and the derived b = 2.897771955… × 10⁻³ m·K. This desk computes b, σ, and a from h, c, and k.

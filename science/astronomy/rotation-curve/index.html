Vol. Science · Astronomy · The flat curve

Rotation curves

Circular speed is the speed of a circular orbit. For a spherical mass it is v(r) = √(G · M(<r) / r). M(<r) is the mass inside radius r. If that mass were a central point, the speed far out would fall as r−1/2. Spiral galaxies do not do that. The curve stays roughly flat, so the enclosed mass grows about linearly with r, which is more mass than the starlight shows. Rubin and Ford 1970 measured 67 H II regions in M31 from 3 to 24 kpc. Their abstract says that beyond 4 kpc the mass increases approximately linearly to 14 kpc, and more slowly after that, and that the mass to 24 kpc is (1.85 ± 0.1) × 1011 solar masses. They wrote that extrapolation beyond that distance is clearly a matter of taste. This page does not refit M31. The cited Sun on the Milky Way button is two later numbers, not a model of that curve: GRAVITY Collaboration 2019, 8178 pc, and Eilers et al. 2019, 229.0 km/s. The period 2πr/v from those two is 219.401 Myr. The unrounded quotient is 219.400731 Myr. Eilers adopted 8.122 kpc, not 8178 pc. They are not averaged. Milgrom 1983 proposed a modified dynamics, MOND, in which a flat curve need not mean unseen mass. This page does not draw that alternative. The map of the disk is the galaxy desk. A stellar wobble is the radial-velocity desk. The shared point of two bodies is the barycenter desk.

What the number is

One speed, one enclosed mass. For a circular orbit in a spherical mass, centripetal acceleration v²/r equals G M(<r) / r², so v² = G M(<r) / r. Turn it around and M(<r) = v² r / G. A flat v means M grows about linearly with r. A point mass does the opposite: M is fixed, so v falls as r−1/2. The dashed curve on the plot is that fall, forced through the toy speed at the probe. It is not a measurement. Rubin and Ford 1970, ApJ 159, 379, is the historical optical curve for M31. The abstract’s (1.85 ± 0.1) × 1011 solar masses to 24 kpc is their disk model, not a slider on this page. The extra mass in the flat picture is the halo. Calling it dark matter is the usual name for mass that does not shine. This page does not identify a particle.

The cited Sun, written out. GRAVITY Collaboration 2019 print R0 = 8178 ± 13 statistical ± 22 systematic pc. 8178 pc is 8.178 kpc. Eilers et al. 2019 print vc(R⊙) = 229.0 ± 0.2 km/s. The abstract puts systematic uncertainties at the ~2–5% level. The discussion puts ~2–3% on the solar value. They are not averaged. Eilers adopted R⊙ = 8.122 ± 0.031 kpc, from Gravity Collaboration 2018. 8.178 − 8.122 = 0.056 kpc. Their slope is −1.7 ± 0.1 km/s per kpc, with a systematic uncertainty of 0.46 km/s per kpc. 0.056 × 1.7 = 0.0952 km/s. This page does not subtract it. 229.0 stays 229.0. One parsec is (648000/π) × 149,597,870,700 m = 3.085677581491 × 1016 m. The radius is 8178 of those metres. The speed is 229,000 m/s. P = 2πR/v. A year is 365.25 × 86,400 s = 31,557,600 s, and 1 Myr is 106 of those years. The unrounded quotient is 219.400731 Myr, which rounds to 219.401 Myr at three decimals. Enclosed mass is v²R/GM☉ with the IAU nominal GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 1020 m³ s−2. That is 9.971419 × 1010 solar masses. The coefficient to twelve decimals is 9.971419375765. Dividing GM☉ by the 2022 CODATA G gives 1.9884098707 × 1030 kg. G’s standard uncertainty, 0.00015 × 10−11, is not folded in. The ±13 pc, the ±22 pc, and the ±0.2 km/s are not folded in either. GRAVITY Collaboration 2021 print 8275 ± 9 statistical ± 33 systematic pc after an aberration correction. This page does not substitute 8275. The galaxy desk already cites the 2019 distance. Its ~8.2 kpc drawing is a different round scale.

What the sliders are. The Milky Way button sets a bulge of 1 × 1010 solar masses and scale 0.5 kpc, a disk of 5 × 1010 solar masses and scale 3 kpc, and a halo with v∞ = 180 km/s and rc = 5 kpc. At 8.178 kpc that toy is 193.039 km/s and 260.273 Myr. 193.039 does not round to 229.0. They are not averaged. The bulge enclosed mass is Mb r³ / (r² + a²)3/2. At a = 0 that is the whole Mb at every r > 0, and the speed falls as r−1/2. The point-mass button is 8 × 1010 solar masses at 8 kpc, which is 207.387 km/s and 236.993 Myr. At 16 kpc the same mass is slower by √2. The disk mass inside cylindrical radius R is Md [1 − exp(−R/Rd) (1 + R/Rd)]. The speed then uses v² = GM/R, as if that cylinder were a sphere. A thin disk’s true circular speed needs a different potential. This page does not compute it. The halo is v² = v∞² (1 − (rc/r) arctan(r/rc)). That is the circular speed of the density ρ0 / (1 + (r/rc)²), with v∞² = 4πGρ0 rc². ρ0 is not a separate slider. The three speeds add in quadrature. The printed components are rounded at three decimals. Adding those rounded squares does not have to return the printed total. Milgrom 1983, ApJ 270, 365, takes the low-acceleration limit a²/a0 ≈ GM/r² as an alternative to unseen mass. This page stays with Newton. It does not adopt an a0 and it does not draw a MOND curve.

What the words mean

Circular speed
v(r) = √(G M(<r) / r), in km/s, with r in kpc. It is the speed of a circular orbit, not the Sun’s peculiar motion and not a radial velocity toward the observer.
Keplerian
The r−1/2 fall of a fixed central mass. The Kepler desk is the same gravity, written as P² = a³ for a circular orbit around one solar mass. The dashed curve here is that fall, matched to the toy at the probe.
Quadrature
v² = vbulge² + vdisk² + vhalo². For these spherical toys that is the same as adding enclosed masses and then taking one speed. An off component contributes zero.
Plummer bulge
M(<r) = Mb r³ / (r² + a²)3/2. The scale a is a classroom length in kpc. a = 0 is a point mass. It is not a fit to a galactic bulge.
Exponential disk
Surface density falling as exp(−R/Rd). The mass inside radius R is Md [1 − exp(−x) (1 + x)], x = R/Rd. This page then pretends that mass is spherical. The thin-disk potential is not drawn.
Pseudo-isothermal halo
v² = v∞² (1 − (rc/r) arctan(r/rc)). The speed rises from zero and levels off near v∞. v∞ and rc are classroom knobs, not a Milky Way fit.
Dark matter
Mass that the flat curve requires if the force is still Newton’s, and that the starlight does not show. The halo slider is that mass in toy form. No particle is named.
MOND
Milgrom 1983, ApJ 270, 365. In the low-acceleration limit the paper writes a²/a0 ≈ GM/r², so a flat outer curve need not mean unseen mass. This page names that alternative and does not draw it. No value of a0 is adopted.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

  • GRAVITY Collaboration 2019, A&A 625, L10 — geometric distance to the Galactic centre, R0 = 8178 ± 13 statistical ± 22 systematic pc. This page’s cited radius. The same paper is on the galaxy source list. The ~8.2 kpc label on that drawing is not 8178 pc.
  • GRAVITY Collaboration 2021, A&A 647, A59 — after an aberration correction, R0 = 8275 ± 9 statistical ± 33 systematic pc. Not substituted.
  • Eilers, Hogg, Rix, and Ness 2019, ApJ 871, 120 — vc(R⊙) = 229.0 ± 0.2 km/s. The abstract prints systematic uncertainties at the ~2–5% level. The discussion prints ~2–3% for the solar value. They adopted R⊙ = 8.122 ± 0.031 kpc from Gravity Collaboration 2018, A&A 615, L15. Their linear fit declines at −1.7 ± 0.1 km/s per kpc, systematic uncertainty 0.46 km/s per kpc. This page uses 229.0 and does not slide it to 8.178 kpc. Preprint: arXiv:1810.09466.
  • Rubin and Ford 1970, ApJ 159, 379 — spectra of 67 H II regions from 3 to 24 kpc in M31. The abstract says the mass beyond 4 kpc increases approximately linearly to 14 kpc, and more slowly thereafter, and that the mass to 24 kpc is (1.85 ± 0.1) × 1011 solar masses. The paper says extrapolation beyond that distance is clearly a matter of taste. Not refit here.
  • Milgrom 1983, ApJ 270, 365 — a modification of the Newtonian dynamics as a possible alternative to the hidden mass hypothesis. The low-acceleration limit in the abstract is a²/a0 ≈ GM/r². Named, not drawn.
  • NIST — Newtonian constant of gravitation — 2022 CODATA recommended value, 6.67430 × 10−11 m³ kg−1 s−2, standard uncertainty 0.00015 × 10−11. The uncertainty is not folded into the mass.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B3 (Prša et al. 2016, AJ 152, 41) — nominal solar mass parameter GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 1020 m³ s−2. A conversion constant, not a new measurement of the Sun. The same constant as the black-hole desk. Divided by the CODATA G it is 1.9884098707 × 1030 kg.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — the astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 m.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B2 — footnote 4: the parsec is defined as exactly (648000/π) au. With the 2012 au, that is 3.085 677 581 … × 1016 m. This page’s value of that product is 3.085677581491 × 1016 m.

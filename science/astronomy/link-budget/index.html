Vol. Science · Astronomy · The link

Deep space
radio link

A spacecraft transmitter is a few tens of watts. The largest Deep Space Network dishes are 70 m across. The power that arrives still falls as 1/d², because the same energy is spread over a sphere. This desk is that arithmetic. Wavelength is c/f. A round dish has gain G = η(πD/λ)². Free-space path loss is (4πd/λ)². Received power is Pr = Pt·Gt·Gr·(λ/4πd)². Noise density is kT. C/N0 is Pr/kT. The rate at a chosen Eb/N0 is that C/N0 divided by the linear ratio. The unlimited-bandwidth Shannon rate is (C/N0)/ln(2). ln(2) is −1.59 dB when rounded to 0.01 dB. The rate uses the unrounded ln(2). The wait for the answer is the light-time desk. A dish as an optical aperture is the telescope desk. A different radio line, the hydrogen spin flip, is the 21 cm desk.

A 70 m dish at 8.4 GHz with η = 0.6 is 73.58 dBi. Path loss at 1 AU at 8.4 GHz is 274.43 dB. The X-band button is 8420 MHz, the 70 m handbook’s receive reference, not 8.4 GHz exactly. At 8420 MHz and η = 0.55 the same dish is 73.22 dBi, and 1 AU is 274.45 dB. DSN 810-005 module 101, Rev. H, quotes DSS-14’s X-only vacuum gain as 74.55 dBi at 8420 MHz. Inverting this formula at 70 m gives η = 0.747. Neither 74.55 nor 0.747 is substituted. η opens at 0.55, a classroom default, not a measured efficiency.

The page opens on Voyager 1. The PDS radio catalog gives a 3.66 m reflector and X-band powers of 12 W and 22 W. This page uses 22 W. The catalog prints 47 dB at X-band. At η = 0.55 the formula prints 47.59 dBi. 47 is not substituted. The distance is one light-day, c × 86,400 s, which NASA Science dates to 18 November 2026, 2:16:07 a.m. PST, and prints as 25.902 billion km. This page does not advance the spacecraft past that instant. At 16.69 K and the example threshold of 0 dB the rate is 1,375 bit/s. The Shannon ceiling is 1,984 bit/s. The light time is 24 h 0 min.

Why the rate collapses

The sphere is the whole story of the distance. A transmitter sends power into a beam. Far away, that beam’s footprint is a patch on a sphere of radius d, so the flux falls as 1/d². Doubling the distance costs 6.02 dB, whatever the frequency. Frequency changes the size of the dishes’ beams, not that square. The path-loss number (4πd/λ)² folds the sphere together with the wavelength so that the two dish gains can stay pure numbers. At 1 AU and 8.4 GHz the loss is 274.43 dB. At Voyager’s light-day, on the 8420 MHz button, it is 319.22 dB.

A bigger dish, or a shorter wavelength, is a narrower beam. G = η(πD/λ)². η is the fraction of the physical area that the radio actually uses. It opens at 0.55. The handbook’s 74.55 dBi, inverted, would be 0.747, and that efficiency is not loaded. Ka-band, 32050 MHz, makes both dishes much stronger than S-band, 2295 MHz, at the same diameters. The 70 m module does not list a Ka-band receive gain. The 34 m beam-waveguide antennas do: module 104 quotes 79.0 dBi at 32050 MHz. This page’s Ka number is still the formula.

Noise sets the rate, not the power alone. k is 1.380649×10^−23 J/K exactly. 10 log10(k) is −228.599 dBW/K/Hz to 0.001 dB, which prints −228.60 to 0.01 dB. Multiply by the system temperature and that is the noise in one hertz. The opening temperature, 16.69 K, is module 101 Table 4 for DSS-14 at X-band, X-only, zenith, 25% cumulative-distribution weather: antenna-microwave 11.65 K plus sky 5.04 K. It is not retuned when you change the band. C/N0 in dB-Hz is received power in dBW minus that density. Divide C/N0 by the linear Eb/N0 and the quotient is a bit rate. The 0 dB button is an example threshold. It is not a coded-link requirement, and it is not a margin borrowed from a mission.

Shannon is the ceiling, not a second mission. With unlimited bandwidth the best rate is (C/N0)/ln(2). At the opening link that is 1,984 bit/s, 1.59 dB kinder than the 0 dB example. If you set Eb/N0 below −1.59 dB, the formula prints a rate above that ceiling. The card says so. The higher number is not achievable. Coding can move a practical threshold toward the floor. This page does not apply a code.

The markers are distances, and two of them bring a radio. The Moon is the fact-sheet mean, 384,400 km. Mars closest and farthest are 54.6 and 401.4 × 10^6 km. Jupiter closest is 588.5 × 10^6 km. Pluto closest is 4,284.7 × 10^6 km. Voyager is the light-day. Pluto’s button also loads New Horizons: Fountain et al. and DeBoy et al. 2004 give a 2.1 m reflector and two 12 W X-band traveling-wave tubes, and a requirement of at least 42 dBic inside 0.3°. 42 dBic is not substituted. At η = 0.55 the formula prints 42.76 dBi, which is a different unit. The Pluto distance is the fact-sheet minimum, not the range on 14 July 2015. NASA’s Voyager spacecraft page says the X-band downlink is 160 bit/s normally and 1.4 kbit/s for a high-rate playback. This page’s 1,375 bit/s is the 0 dB formula. It is not that playback rate.

Terms

Gain
G = η(πD/λ)², then 10 log10 for dBi. The same η is applied to both dishes. A real spacecraft dish and a real 70 m dish do not share one efficiency. The handbook gains are quoted beside the formula and are not substituted.
Path loss
(4πd/λ)². It grows with distance and shrinks with wavelength. At 1 AU and 8.4 GHz it is 274.43 dB. The X-band button, 8420 MHz, is 274.45 dB at the same AU.
Received power
Pr = Pt·Gt·Gr·(λ/4πd)². Printed in dBm and in watts. The opening link is −154.99 dBm, 3.168×10^−19 W.
C/N0 and Eb/N0
C/N0 is Pr/(kT), in hertz, printed in dB-Hz. Eb/N0 is the energy per bit over the noise density. The rate is C/N0 divided by that ratio. 0 dB means the ratio is 1, so the rate equals C/N0 in bit/s. That 0 dB is an example.
Shannon ceiling
(C/N0)/ln(2). The matching Eb/N0 is ln(2), −1.59 dB to 0.01 dB. A threshold below that floor asks for a rate the unlimited-bandwidth channel cannot carry.
What this leaves out
Atmospheric loss, rain, the elevation curve of a real dish, pointing loss, polarization mismatch, cable and feed loss, the modulation, and coding gain. The 16.69 K figure already includes a clear-sky zenith contribution for one X-band configuration. It is not a weather model. One-way light time is d/c, the same division as the light-time desk, with no Shapiro delay. Voyager’s 8415 MHz nominal carrier is not the 8420 MHz button. A New Horizons channel frequency is not loaded.

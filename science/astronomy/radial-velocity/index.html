Radial velocity

A planet and its star orbit their shared barycenter. The part of the star’s motion aimed at us shifts the spectral lines: blueshift on the way in, redshift on the way out. The size of that shift is the semi-amplitude K = (2πG/P)1/3 · mp sin i / (M* + mp)2/3 / √(1 − e²). Sun–Jupiter, from the fact-sheet masses, the sidereal period 4,332.589 days, and e = 0.0487, seen edge-on, computes 12.47 m/s. Sun–Earth computes 0.0895 m/s, which is 8.95 cm/s. Only m sin i is fixed by K. The transit desk is the circular case of this speed, plus the dip. Kepler’s year is the period when one mass is the Sun. For e > 0 the curve solves Kepler’s equation. The same √(GM/r) for a whole galaxy, flat instead of Keplerian, is the rotation-curve desk.

Classroom Doppler model, not a measured orbit and not a detection. G is the 2022 CODATA central value, 6.67430 × 10−11 m³ kg−1 s−2. A solar mass, an Earth mass, and a Jupiter mass are the NASA NSSDCA fact-sheet masses. The sheet’s GM is not substituted. The drawn loop is a few seconds per orbit, not the cited period. The ellipse is the star’s path around the barycenter, scaled to the box. The planet’s distance is not that scale. Instrument lines are the published accuracy or requirement, not a false-alarm threshold for the star in the box.

What the signal is

Doppler shift
A star moving toward the observer compresses the wavelengths of its spectral lines. A star moving away stretches them. For speeds much smaller than light, the fractional shift is v/c. The curve on this page is that v, along the line of sight. The lines themselves are the spectra desk. The shift, as a redshift, is the redshift desk.
m sin i
Inclination i is the tilt of the orbit. Edge-on is 90°, and the whole wobble is aimed at us. Face-on is 0°, and the line-of-sight speed is zero. K fixes the product mp sin i, not the planet’s mass by itself. A measured K with an unknown tilt is a minimum mass. The true mass is larger when the orbit is not edge-on.
Hot Jupiters first
A Jupiter-mass planet on a few-day orbit makes a K of tens of meters per second. 51 Pegasi b is that case, the first planet found around a Sun-like star, by this wobble, in 1995. An Earth-mass planet on a one-year orbit, the Sun–Earth row, is 8.95 cm/s. The large, fast signal is the one early Doppler surveys could see. The exoplanets desk is the wider set of methods.
Eccentricity
For a circle, the curve is a sine. For e > 0 the star moves faster near periastron. This page solves M = E − e sin E, then v = K [cos(ν + ω) + e cos ω]. The peaks are not equal. K itself is still the semi-amplitude: the peak-to-peak span is 2K. ω chooses which peak is the fast one. It does not change K. The desk stops at e = 0.9.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

  • NIST — Newtonian constant of gravitation — 2022 CODATA recommended value, 6.67430 × 10−11 m³ kg−1 s−2, standard uncertainty 0.00015 × 10−11. This page multiplies that G by the printed masses. The fact-sheet GM is not substituted.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Sun Fact Sheet — Mass 1,988,400 × 1024 kg. The printed Sun/Earth mass ratio is 332,900. That ratio is not the unit. GM 132,712 × 106 km³/s² is not substituted.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Earth Fact Sheet — Mass 5.9722 × 1024 kg. Sidereal orbit period 365.256 days. Orbit eccentricity 0.0167. Orbit inclination 0.000° is to Earth’s ecliptic. The edge-on preset uses 90° and does not substitute 0.000°.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Jupiter Fact Sheet — Mass 1,898.13 × 1024 kg. Sidereal orbit period 4,332.589 days. Orbit eccentricity 0.0487. Orbit inclination 1.304° is to the ecliptic. The edge-on preset uses 90° and does not substitute 1.304°.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — the astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 m. Used only to report the period-derived axis in AU.
  • NASA Exoplanet Archive — Planetary Systems Composite Parameters (PSCompPars), the default row. 51 Peg b is cited there to Cont et al. 2026. Proxima Cen b is cited there to Suárez Mascareño et al. 2025. GJ 436 b is cited there to Maciejewski et al. 2014. The stellar K on each row is listed beside this page’s arithmetic. It is not substituted.
  • ESO — HARPS overview — built for a long-term radial-velocity accuracy on the order of 1 m/s. A global accuracy of about 1 m/s RMS is stated for spectral types later than G and v sin i under 2 km/s. This page quotes that figure. It is not a noise model for the star in the box.
  • ESO — ESPRESSO overview — the 1-UT high-resolution mode lists a radial-velocity precision requirement below 10 cm/s. That cell is a requirement, not a demonstrated floor for every target.

The formula

What each symbol is on this desk
K(2πG/P)^(1/3) · m_p sin i / (M* + m_p)^(2/3) / √(1−e²), in m/s. Positive on the curve is recession, a redshift.
G2022 CODATA, 6.67430 × 10^−11. The fact-sheet GM is not substituted.
M*, m_pSolar, Earth, and Jupiter masses are the NSSDCA sheets. An archive MJup or M⊕ is multiplied by that sheet mass.
PThe period, with a day of 86,400 seconds.
eEccentricity, from 0 to 0.9. The shape solves M = E − e sin E.
i0° is face-on and silent. 90° is edge-on. K carries sin i, so the weighed mass is m sin i.
ωArgument of periastron. It slides the fast peak. It does not change K.

Vol. Science · Astronomy · Sunlight and the gap

Temperatures

A world’s sunlight-only temperature is not the temperature a thermometer reads. Absorbed sunlight is the solar luminosity times (1 − A), spread over a sphere of radius the orbital distance. For a fast rotator that shares the heat over the whole globe, and radiates with emissivity 1, Teq = [L (1 − A) / (16πσd²)]1/4. L is the NASA Sun Fact Sheet luminosity, 382.8 × 1024 W. A is the Bond albedo on each NASA NSSDCA sheet. The same 4 is the full-redistribution formula on the habitable zone desk. The sheet’s black-body temperature and the sheet’s surface or 1-bar temperature stay beside this arithmetic. They are not averaged. Earth’s arithmetic is 255.1 K. The Earth sheet prints 254.0 K and an average of 288 K. Venus’s average, 737 K, sits 510.4 K above its black-body temperature. A giant planet’s warmer 1-bar temperature is not that kind of gap.

Classroom energy-balance model, not a climate model. Not a weather forecast and not an ephemeris. The drawing is sunlight in, a reflected fraction, and infrared out. The greenhouse blanket is a picture. It does not add a term. The slow-rotator control replaces the 4 with a 2 and is still only sunlight. Colors are a key, not photographs. A dash means that sheet does not print the value.

What the gap is

Albedo throws sunlight away before it can warm anything. Bond albedo is the fraction of incident sunlight reflected, over all wavelengths and all angles. The fact-sheet notes call it planetary albedo. Geometric albedo is a different column on the same sheets and is not used here. A higher Bond albedo lowers Teq. Venus’s 0.77 is why its sunlight-only temperature is cooler than Earth’s even though Venus is closer to the Sun.

The 4 and the 2 are two classroom divisors. The 4 is a fast rotator, or any world that shares the absorbed heat over the whole surface: a cross-section absorbs, a sphere emits. The 2 is a slow rotator that emits only from the dayside hemisphere. Replacing 4 with 2 multiplies the temperature by 21/4. The sheet’s black-body temperature is compared with the 4. It is not recomputed when the slow button is on. Mercury’s sunward side, 590 to 725 K, is a measured range on that sheet. It is not this page’s slow-rotator number.

A greenhouse gap is measured temperature minus the sunlight-only number. The fact-sheet notes define black-body temperature as the surface temperature the body would have in radiative equilibrium with no atmosphere, but the same albedo. Earth’s sheet prints 288 K and 254.0 K. This arithmetic is 255.1 K. 288 minus 254.0 is 34. 288 minus 255.1 is 32.9. Venus prints 737 K beside 226.6 K, a difference of 510.4 K. Mars prints ~214 K beside 209.8 K, a difference of 4.2 K, and the tilde stays on the 214. The Moon’s sheet prints no average, so that gap is a dash. The blanket on the drawing is only the idea of infrared turned back.

A giant planet’s warmer 1-bar temperature is a different leftover. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune print a temperature at 1 bar, not an average surface temperature. Each 1-bar figure is warmer than that sheet’s black-body temperature. NASA Science (17 July 2025) says Jupiter, Saturn, and Neptune each emit more energy than they receive from the Sun, and that Uranus releases about 15% more energy than it receives, far less than Neptune, which emits more than twice the energy it receives. Those ratios are an energy budget. They are not this page’s kelvin subtraction, and the subtraction is not a greenhouse excess.

What the words mean

Equilibrium temperature
Teq = [L (1−A) / (16πσd²)]1/4 for the fast rotator. L is 382.8 × 1024 W. d is the distance in metres, using 1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km from the fact-sheet notes. σ is computed from the defined h, c, and k, the same expression as the blackbody desk. Emissivity is 1. There is no greenhouse term.
The 2
The slow rotator replaces 16π with 8π, which is the factor 2 instead of 4 in T4 = F(1−A) / (nσ). The dayside hemisphere emits. The night side, in this idealization, does not. It is still sunlight only.
Bond albedo
The fraction of incident sunlight reflected back into space without absorption. Earth 0.294, Venus 0.77, Mars 0.250, Mercury 0.068, the Moon 0.11, Jupiter 0.343, Saturn 0.342, Uranus 0.300, Neptune 0.290, Pluto 0.72. Geometric albedo is not substituted for any of these.
Black-body temperature
The fact-sheet notes: the surface temperature the body would have if it were in radiative equilibrium and had no atmosphere, but the same albedo. It is the sheet’s number. This page’s arithmetic is a second number. Where they differ at the printed tenth, both stay.
Greenhouse
An atmosphere can let sunlight in and hold infrared on the way out, so the surface runs warmer than the sunlight-only temperature. Earth’s gap and Venus’s gap are that idea, read off the sheets. This page does not compute an optical depth. The blanket button only draws the idea.
What this balance leaves out
Clouds, oceans, a real gas mixture, latitude, the seasons on the obliquity desk, and the exobase temperature on the which gases desk. Internal heat on Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, cited above as an energy ratio and not converted into kelvin here. The frost line is a temperature in a young disk, not this equilibrium temperature. Not a climate model.

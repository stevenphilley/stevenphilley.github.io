Vol. Science · Astronomy · The critical distance

Roche
inside this distance, a moon shreds

A moon that comes too close can be pulled apart, and the pieces can stay in orbit as a ring. The fluid Roche limit, the classic spherical form, is d ≈ 2.44 R · (ρprimary / ρsatellite)1/3. The rigid-body variant uses 1.26 in place of 2.44. Read them as a band, not as one magic line. Presets use NASA NSSDCA fact-sheet mean densities, volumetric mean radii, and mean orbital distances. For Saturn and Rhea the fluid distance is 116,698 km (2.004 volumetric radii) and the rigid distance is 60,262 km (1.035). Rhea’s semimajor axis is 527,040 km, outside both. Earth–Moon gives a fluid distance of 18,364 km; the Moon’s semimajor axis is 384,400 km. Mars–Phobos gives fluid 10,541 km and rigid 5,443 km; Phobos’s mean orbital distance is 9,378 km, between them. The tides desk is the near-side and far-side difference. The Lagrange desk is the five points that keep station.

When the moon is far outside, the lower panel magnifies the neighborhood of the primary, and that panel has its own scale. The satellite mark is not the moon’s radius. Not leftover paint

A ring is material that cannot become a moon

Rings are not paint left over from the planet’s making, sitting there because nobody cleaned up. Inside the Roche distance, loose material cannot gather into a moon. A body that wanders in can be pulled apart, and the pieces stay in orbit as a ring. Saturn’s bright rings are that kind of material. Rhea is not. Its mean orbit is hundreds of thousands of kilometers outside both limits computed for its density. The Moon is farther outside still. Phobos is the awkward one: the fact-sheet orbit sits between the rigid distance and the fluid one, and Phobos is irregular and a rubble body, so the formulas are a classroom comparison, not a forecast. The tides desk is a gentler version of the same unequal pull. The gravity desk is the pull itself.

The formulas

d ≈ 2.44 R · (ρprimary / ρsatellite)1/3. This is the fluid Roche limit in the classic spherical form. R is the primary’s volumetric mean radius. The densities are the fact-sheet means, in kg/m³, so the ratio has no units. A fluid moon has no strength. Inside this distance its own gravity cannot hold it together against the primary’s tide. The coefficient 2.44 already assumes a sphere. It is a classroom constant, not a fitted measurement from a spacecraft.

d ≈ 1.26 R · (ρprimary / ρsatellite)1/3. The same cube root, with 1.26 in place of 2.44. This is the rigid-body variant: a body that can bear some stress, still idealized as a sphere. It sits closer to the primary than the fluid distance. The gap between the two numbers is the band. A moon outside the fluid distance is outside both. A moon inside the rigid distance is inside both. A moon between them is inside the fluid limit and outside the rigid one.

The inputs. Saturn: volumetric mean radius 58,232 km, mean density 687 kg/m³. Rhea: mean density 1,240 kg/m³, semimajor axis 527.04 × 10³ km. Earth: volumetric mean radius 6,371.000 km, mean density 5,513 kg/m³. The Moon fact sheet’s Earth column prints 5,514; this desk uses the Earth Fact Sheet’s 5,513. Moon: mean density 3,344 kg/m³, semimajor axis 0.3844 × 106 km. Mars: volumetric mean radius 3,389.5 km, mean density 3,934 kg/m³. Phobos: mean density 1,900 kg/m³, mean orbital distance 9,378 km. The slider clamps the density ratio from 0.25 to 4 and leaves the orbit and the primary radius where the sheets put them. Equal density sets the ratio to 1.

Saturn’s rings, as a comparison. The rings fact sheet lists the D ring’s inner edge at 66,900 km and the A ring’s outer edge at 136,780 km. Those kilometers are from Saturn’s center. The same sheet’s “Saturn radii” column divides by the equatorial radius 60,268 km, so the A edge is 2.270 equatorial radii. This desk’s R is the volumetric mean, 58,232 km, so the same edge is 2.349 volumetric radii. For Rhea’s density the fluid distance is 116,698 km, and the outer A ring sits outside that number. For equal density the fluid distance is 2.44 × 58,232 km = 142,086 km, and the A edge sits inside it. The classroom point is that icy material near Saturn cannot gather into a moon. It is not that every ring particle has Rhea’s density.

Where this shows up

Saturn and Rhea. Rhea’s semimajor axis is 527,040 km, which the moon sheet also prints as 8.7449 Saturn radii using the equatorial radius. In volumetric radii that orbit is 9.051 R, far outside both limits for Rhea’s density. The axes on the sheet are 765 × 763 × 762 km. This page’s 763 km figure is the cube root of that product, labeled as derived. The ring band on the drawing is the fact-sheet span, drawn to the same scale as the arcs.

Earth and the Moon. The fluid distance is 18,364 km, 2.882 Earth radii. The rigid distance is 9,483 km, 1.488 Earth radii. The Moon’s semimajor axis is 384,400 km, 60.336 Earth radii. The Moon is not close. The sheet also says the distance from Earth ranges roughly from 357,000 km to 407,000 km. Even the near end of that range is far outside 18,364 km. The Moon’s own volumetric mean radius, 1,737.4 km, is not an input to the formula. It is printed so the moon has a size on the page.

Mars and Phobos. The fluid distance is 10,541 km, 3.110 Mars radii. The rigid distance is 5,443 km, 1.606 Mars radii. Phobos’s mean orbital distance is 9,378 km, 2.767 Mars radii. That is inside the fluid limit and outside the rigid one: between. The sheet gives three axes, 13.0 × 11.4 × 9.1 km, and a mean density of 1,900 kg/m³. Phobos is irregular. The formulas assume a sphere. Treat the verdict as a schematic comparison of those published means, not as a claim that Phobos is about to come apart.

Literacy · a band, not a line

Roche limit
The distance inside which a satellite cannot hold together against the primary’s tide. This page prints two of them. The fluid number uses 2.44. The rigid number uses 1.26. Both scale with the cube root of the density ratio.
Fluid
A strengthless sphere. Its own gravity is the only thing holding it. The classic equal-density case, ratio 1, puts the fluid limit at 2.44 primary radii. For Saturn’s volumetric radius that is 142,086 km, outside the A ring’s 136,780 km edge.
Rigid
The same cube root, closer in, for a body that can bear some stress. Still a sphere in the formula. Real rubble, spin, and internal strength move the true distance. This page does not add a strength model.
Inside, between, outside
Outside: the mean orbit is beyond the fluid distance, so it is beyond both. Between: inside the fluid distance and outside the rigid one. Inside: inside the rigid distance, so inside both. Phobos, on the fact-sheet ratio, is between. Rhea and the Moon are outside.
Density ratio
ρprimary / ρsatellite. Saturn over Rhea is 687 / 1,240. Earth over the Moon is 5,513 / 3,344. Mars over Phobos is 3,934 / 1,900. The slider runs from 0.25 to 4. Equal density is 1. Moving it does not move the moon. It only moves the two arcs.
What the circles leave out
Eccentricity, the barycenter, spin, internal strength, and every other body. Orbits are drawn as circles centered on the primary. Body disks use the volumetric mean radius; the satellite mark does not. Saturn’s band is a fact-sheet radial span, not a picture of the rings. Phobos is rubble and irregular. The lower panel, when it appears, has its own scale. Mean densities, compressed where a moon is far away. Not an ephemeris. Not a mission tool.

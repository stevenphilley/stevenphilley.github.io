Orion Arm

Local Spur · Sol’s neighborhood

A minor spur between the Sagittarius–Carina and Perseus arms. Home to the Sun, nearby star-forming regions (Orion molecular clouds), and most naked-eye stars you learn first.

Bars are logarithmic-ish for readability, not a strict log plot. 1 kpc ≈ 3,260 light-years, so 8.2 kpc is about 27,000 ly. The ~26,000 ly row is the usual round number for that distance. Thin-disc scale height is about 300 pc (~1,000 ly), which is why the edge-on view is a thin band across a ~100,000 ly disc. A fainter thick-disc layer uses about 900 pc.

Sources · scale

Where the numbers come from

  • GRAVITY Collaboration 2019, A&A 625, L10 — geometric distance to Sgr A*, R0 = 8178 ± 13 ± 22 pc.
  • Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016, ARA&A 54, 529 — the round scale used here: R0 = 8.2 ± 0.1 kpc, the Sun 25 ± 5 pc above the stellar midplane, thin-disc scale height 300 ± 50 pc, scale length 2.6 ± 0.5 kpc.
  • Reid et al. 2019, ApJ 885, 131 — BeSSeL / VERA maser parallaxes. 3D arm pitch and radii follow their log-spiral fits, kinks included. They find R0 = 8.15 ± 0.15 kpc and a smaller vertical offset, Z⊙ = 5.5 ± 5.8 pc, on the young-star plane.
  • Wegg, Gerhard & Portail 2015, MNRAS 450, 4050 — long bar half-length 5.0 ± 0.2 kpc, angle to the Sun–center line 28–33°. This drawing uses 5 kpc and 30°.
  • NASA Imagine the Universe — The Milky Way — classroom diameter about 100,000 light-years. Sgr A* is about four million solar masses.

Glossary · starter terms

Spiral arm
Denser lane of stars, gas, and dust where star formation is more active — a traffic jam in the disk’s density wave, not a rigid spoke.
Orion Arm / Local Spur
Our minor arm segment. “Local Spur” emphasizes it is not a grand-design major arm like Perseus or Scutum–Centaurus.
Galactic center
~8.2 kpc (~26,000 ly) toward Sagittarius. Hosts the radio source Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole of about four million solar masses.
Bar
Elongated stellar structure through the inner galaxy; the Milky Way is a barred spiral (SBc-ish).
Halo
Sparse spherical population of old stars and dark matter surrounding the disk — globular clusters live here.
kpc / ly
Kiloparsec and light-year. Astronomers mix both; educational pages often translate to ly for intuition.
Plane / latitude
The Milky Way band in the night sky is the disk edge-on. Galactic latitude b = 0° hugs that plane.
Gaia
ESA space observatory mapping stellar positions and motions — the modern backbone for arm maps. This page is a cartoon, not Gaia data.

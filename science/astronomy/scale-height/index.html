Scale height

An isothermal atmosphere thins by the same fraction in every step of height. The step is the scale height, H = kT / (m g) = R T / (M g). Pressure follows P(z) = P0 · exp(−z/H). One H up, the pressure is 1/e of the pressure at the reference level. T is the NASA NSSDCA average temperature, or the temperature at 1 bar. M is the sheet’s mean molecular weight. g is the sheet’s mean gravity. Earth’s arithmetic is 8.417 km. The Earth sheet prints 8.5 km. They are not averaged. The temperature on the temperatures desk is a sunlight balance. It is not this T. Which gases can leave the top of the column is the Jeans escape desk.

Classroom isothermal model, not a climate model. Not a weather forecast and not an ephemeris. The drawing is pressure against altitude, on a logarithmic pressure axis, so the exponential is a straight line. The dashed verticals are e-folding heights. Colors are a key, not photographs. A dash means that sheet does not print the value.

What the height is

Isothermal means one temperature at every height. The fact-sheet notes define scale height as the height interval in which the atmospheric pressure changes by a factor of e. In an isothermal ideal gas that interval is H = kT/(m g). A real atmosphere has a lapse rate, so the local scale height changes with height. This page uses one temperature, the sheet’s average or the sheet’s 1-bar temperature, and does not add a gradient. Where the arithmetic does not round to the printed scale height, both numbers stay.

Titan’s low gravity puffs a column up. Scale height is inversely proportional to g. The Saturnian Satellite Fact Sheet prints Titan’s mass, 1,345.5 × 1020 kg, and radius, 2,575 km. It does not print a surface gravity, a temperature, a mean molecular weight, or a scale height. This page’s gravity is GM/r² = 1.354 m/s². Earth’s mean surface gravity is 9.820 m/s². The same temperature and the same molecular weight give Titan a scale height 7.251 times Earth’s. At Earth’s 288 K and 28.97 that is 61.030 km beside Earth’s 8.417 km. That curve is the low g. It is not a Titan atmosphere read off the sheet.

A giant planet’s numbers are quoted at the 1-bar level. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune have no solid surface on their sheets. Gravity is “mean, 1 bar.” Temperature is “at 1 bar.” The printed scale height is still one interval, and this formula still uses that one temperature. Jupiter’s arithmetic is 23.841 km beside 27 km. Saturn’s is 48.099 km beside 59.5 km. Uranus’s is 26.566 km beside 27.7 km. Neptune’s molecular weight is the range 2.53–2.69 and the scale height is 19.1–20.3 km. The two ends are 20.995 km and 19.747 km. They are not averaged with the listed range. The magnetospheres desk is a different boundary, where the solar wind stands off.

What the words mean

Scale height
H = kT/(m g). k is 1.380649 × 10−23 J/K. m is the mean molecular weight times the atomic mass constant. g is the mean gravity. The same number is R T/(M g), with M the molar mass in kg/mol, because the notes call a dalton equivalent to grams per mole. The two forms agree at the thousandth of a kilometre this page prints.
Barometric formula
P(z) = P0 exp(−z/H), isothermal. At z = H, 2H, and 3H the pressure ratio is 1/e, 1/e², and 1/e³. 1/e is 0.3679. The notes print e = 2.7183. This page’s exp uses the mathematical constant. The chart’s vertical axis is logarithmic, so the curve is a straight line.
Mean molecular weight
The notes: mean relative molecular mass of the atmospheric constituents, in daltons, equivalent to grams per mole. Earth 28.97, Venus 43.45, Mars 43.49, Jupiter 2.22, Saturn 2.07, Uranus 2.64. Neptune is the range 2.53–2.69. This page does not pick a midpoint.
1-bar level
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune print gravity and temperature at 1 bar. The notes say a gas giant’s gravity is given at that level. The comparison-table note says the mean temperature of a gas giant is at the one bar level. There is no solid surface to hang a “surface” scale height on.
Titan
Mass 1,345.5 × 1020 kg and radius 2,575 km. Gravity on this page is the 2022 CODATA G times that mass, divided by the square of that radius: 1.354 m/s². The sheet does not print g, T, M, or H. The button keeps whatever temperature and molecular weight the sliders already have.
What this model leaves out
A lapse rate, condensation, the difference between the equatorial acceleration and the mean gravity, and the exobase temperature on the Jeans escape desk. Earth’s exobase there is 1000 K. The temperature in this formula is 288 K. The temperatures desk’s 255.1 K is a sunlight-only balance. It is not H’s temperature either. Not a climate model.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

  • NASA NSSDCA — Notes on the fact sheet parameters — updated 6 May 2024. Scale height: the height interval in which the atmospheric pressure changes by a factor of e = 2.7183. Mean molecular weight: mean relative molecular mass in daltons, equivalent to grams per mole. Surface gravity (mean): GM over the square of the volumetric mean radius, at the surface or the 1-bar level, not including rotation. 1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km is on the same page and is not used here.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Planetary Fact Sheet notes — updated 20 December 2021. For the gas giants the gravity is given at the 1 bar pressure level. Mean temperature for the gas giants is at the one bar level. The surfaces of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune are deep in the atmosphere and the location and pressures are not known.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Planetary Fact Sheet — comparison table, updated 13 November 2026. Gravity in m/s²: Venus 8.9, Earth 9.8, Mars 3.7, Jupiter 23.1, Saturn 9.0, Uranus 8.7, Neptune 11.0. Those cells are coarser than the individual sheets. This page does not use them. The table does not list a scale height or a mean molecular weight.
  • Venus Fact Sheet — updated 11 January 2024. Surface gravity (mean) 8.87 m/s². Scale height 15.9 km. Average temperature 737 K. Mean molecular weight 43.45.
  • Earth Fact Sheet — updated 15 November 2024. Surface gravity (mean) 9.820 m/s². Scale height 8.5 km. Average temperature 288 K. Mean molecular weight 28.97. The defined standard acceleration is given as CODATA 2018, 9.80665 m/s² exact. This formula uses 9.820.
  • Mars Fact Sheet — updated 19 May 2025. Surface gravity (mean) 3.73 m/s². Scale height 11.0 km. Average temperature ~214 K. Mean molecular weight 43.49. The tilde stays on 214.
  • Jupiter Fact Sheet — updated 2 October 2024. Gravity (mean, 1 bar) 25.92 m/s². Scale height 27 km. Temperature at 1 bar 165 K. Mean molecular weight 2.22.
  • Saturn Fact Sheet — updated 18 March 2025. Gravity (mean, 1 bar) 11.19 m/s². Scale height 59.5 km. Temperature at 1 bar 134 K. Mean molecular weight 2.07.
  • Uranus Fact Sheet — updated 2 October 2024. Gravity (mean, 1 bar) 9.01 m/s². Scale height 27.7 km. Temperature at 1 bar 76 K. Mean molecular weight 2.64.
  • Neptune Fact Sheet — updated 3 October 2024. Gravity (mean, 1 bar) 11.27 m/s². Scale height 19.1–20.3 km. Temperature at 1 bar 72 K. Mean molecular weight 2.53–2.69. Both ends stay.
  • Saturnian Satellite Fact Sheet — updated 22 July 2025. Titan mass 1,345.5 × 1020 kg, radius 2,575 km, mean density 1880 kg/m³. No scale height, no mean molecular weight, no temperature, and no surface gravity. This page multiplies the mass by the 2022 CODATA G, the same G the Jeans escape desk uses for Titan.
  • NIST CODATA — Boltzmann constant — k = 1.380649 × 10−23 J/K, exact. Atomic mass constant u = 1.66053906892 × 10−27 kg, 2022 CODATA. Molar gas constant R = 8.314462618 J mol−1 K−1. Newtonian constant of gravitation G = 6.67430 × 10−11 m³ kg−1 s−2, 2022 CODATA, used only for Titan’s GM/r². m = M × u, with M the sheet’s mean molecular weight in daltons.

Moon
phases

Set a date and a clock, drag the dial, or press Now. The desk shows the Moon’s phase at that moment — name, age, and the geometric fraction of the disk in sunlight. Pair with tides (new and full line up with spring tides; the quarters are neap), the Campbell / Bay Area local sky, and the light pollution desk when planning faint-object nights.

Phase instants follow Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (2nd ed., ch. 49). The mean synodic month for the year 2000 is 29.530588 days (Fred Espenak, NASA GSFC). The real Moon also librates, and its orbit is tilted about 5° — so a new or full Moon is not an eclipse. That geometry has its own desk: Eclipses.

Moon

Half the near side lit. Good for crater shadows along the terminator — the line between day and night on the Moon.

Handy numbers

Synodic month
29.530588 days in the year 2000 — new Moon to new Moon. One month can run about 29.26 to 29.80 days.
Sidereal month
~27.3 days — one orbit relative to the stars; shorter than the phase cycle.
Mean distance
~384,400 km · about 30 Earth diameters · light travel ~1.3 s.
Diameter
~3,475 km · roughly 1/4 Earth’s · gravity ~1/6 g on the surface.
Terminator
Day/night line on the Moon — best contrast for craters near first/last quarter.
Earthshine
Near new, the dark disk can glow faintly from sunlight reflected off Earth.

Observing from the South Bay

Moon vs deep sky

  • Near full: great for maria and bright rays; terrible for galaxies and dim nebulae — skyglow rises hard, especially under already-bright Campbell / San Jose skies.
  • Near new / thin crescent: best window for the Milky Way band and faint fuzzies if haze and local lights cooperate — see light pollution.
  • First / last quarter: sweet spot for lunar topography (long shadows) without maximum washout.
  • Check rise/set against your local sky plan; hills west of SR-17 and marine layer timing still win or lose the night.

Sources

Cited, not invented

  • Fred Espenak, NASA GSFC — Phases of the Moon, 2001 to 2025 — for the year 2000 the mean synodic month is 29.530588 days (29d 12h 44m 03s). Any one synodic month can run from about 29.26 to 29.80 days.
  • Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed. (Willmann-Bell, 1998), chapter 49 — times of new moon, first quarter, full moon, and last quarter. Those instants are Terrestrial Time. This page converts them with the ΔT polynomials Espenak published for the Five Millennium Canon: Polynomial Expressions for Delta T.
  • U.S. Naval Observatory — Dates of Primary Phases of the Moon — the check. On sample new and full moons in 1969, 2000, 2024, and 2026, the Meeus instant falls within two minutes of the USNO minute.
  • Jean Meeus, same book, chapter 48 — illuminated fraction of the disk, (1 + cos i) / 2, with the phase angle measured from full. Here that is (1 − cos φ) / 2 with φ measured from new. Between the four phase instants, φ is interpolated in time. Not a libration portrait, and not a rise or set time.

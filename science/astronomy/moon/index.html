Moon
phases

Drag the dial (or use presets) to walk a synodic month — new through full and back. Illumination here is a simple geometric cartoon for learning names and sky brightness, not a precision ephemeris. Pair with tides (new and full line up with spring tides; the quarters are neap), the Campbell / Bay Area local sky, and the light pollution desk when planning faint-object nights.

Phase angle ≈ age / 29.53 × 360°. The real Moon also librates, and its orbit is tilted about 5° — so a new or full Moon is not an eclipse. That geometry has its own desk: Eclipses. Figures round NASA educational orders of magnitude.

Handy numbers

Synodic month
~29.53 days — new Moon to new Moon as seen from Earth (phase cycle).
Sidereal month
~27.3 days — one orbit relative to the stars; shorter than the phase cycle.
Mean distance
~384,400 km · about 30 Earth diameters · light travel ~1.3 s.
Diameter
~3,475 km · roughly 1/4 Earth’s · gravity ~1/6 g on the surface.
Terminator
Day/night line on the Moon — best contrast for craters near first/last quarter.
Earthshine
Near new, the dark disk can glow faintly from sunlight reflected off Earth.

Observing from the South Bay

Moon vs deep sky

  • Near full: great for maria and bright rays; terrible for galaxies and dim nebulae — skyglow rises hard, especially under already-bright Campbell / San Jose skies.
  • Near new / thin crescent: best window for the Milky Way band and faint fuzzies if haze and local lights cooperate — see light pollution.
  • First / last quarter: sweet spot for lunar topography (long shadows) without maximum washout.
  • Check rise/set against your local sky plan; hills west of SR-17 and marine layer timing still win or lose the night.

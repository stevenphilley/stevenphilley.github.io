Vol. Science · Astronomy · The burn

Oberth
effect

The same impulsive Δv does not buy the same orbital energy everywhere. Specific energy is ε = v²/2 − μ/r. On a bound Keplerian ellipse that is also −μ/(2a). A burn along the velocity vector changes the speed from v to v + Δv, so the energy changes by Δε = v·Δv + ½(Δv)². The cross term is the Oberth effect: a larger v, deep in the well, multiplies the same Δv into a larger energy gain. The ½(Δv)² term does not care how fast the craft was already going. Start on a circle, vc = √(μ/r). A prograde burn leaves periapsis at the burn point. If the new energy is still negative, a' = −μ/(2ε') and the apoapsis is 2a' − r. If ε' ≥ 0 the craft is unbound. Escape from the circle takes Δvesc = vc(√2 − 1), the gap between the two speeds on the Escape desk. Around Earth, μ is the Earth Fact Sheet GM, 0.39860 × 106 km3/s2, and the volumetric mean radius is 6,371.000 km. At altitude 0 those two figures give 11.186 km/s, the escape velocity the sheet lists. A classroom circle 400 km up has r = 6,771 km, circular speed 7.673 km/s, local escape 10.851 km/s, and Δv to escape 3.178 km/s. The 400 km altitude is an example. The fact sheet does not print it. It is the low orbit on the Hohmann and vis-viva desks. The comparison circle at the geosynchronous radius is 42,164.190 km, from r³ = μ T² / (4π²) with the sheet’s sidereal rotation period, 23.9345 hours. That is 35,793.190 km above the volumetric radius. It is a classroom circle at that r, not a mission. A 1 km/s burn gains 8.173 km²/s² at 400 km and 3.575 km²/s² on that higher circle. Speed on the ellipse after the burn is vis-viva. The half-ellipse between two circles is Hohmann. The year is Kepler.

Classroom schematic. Point-mass, two-body, one impulsive burn along the velocity. No atmosphere, no drag, no oblateness, and no third body. Not an ephemeris and not a mission planner. Top-down, from the north, so a prograde burn points counterclockwise. The two circles are to scale with each other. When the low circle hugs the body, the body is drawn smaller so the gap stays visible, and the hint says so. The 400 km and 10,000 km altitudes are classroom examples. The geosynchronous radius is computed from the fact sheet. It is not an altitude the sheet prints. The solar preset uses Earth’s semimajor axis and Mars’s semimajor axis as the radii of two circles. Those fact-sheet orbits are not circles.

What the formula is

ε = v²/2 − μ/r. μ is GM of the central body. r is the distance from that body. v is the speed. On a circle, vc = √(μ/r), so ε = −μ/(2r). On a bound ellipse the same energy is −μ/(2a). The Escape desk is the two speeds at one r: circular, and √2 times circular. The vis-viva desk is the speed the ellipse already has, v = √[μ (2/r − 1/a)].

Δε = v·Δv + ½(Δv)² for an impulse along the velocity. The new speed is v' = v + Δv. Expanding v'²/2 − v²/2 gives the cross term and the square. The cross term grows with v. The square does not. That is why the same burn, deep in the well, changes the energy more than it does higher up, where the circular speed is lower.

From a circle, a prograde burn puts periapsis at the burn point. If ε' is negative, a' = −μ/(2ε') and ra = 2a' − r. If ε' is zero, the path is a parabola and the excess speed at infinity is zero. If ε' is positive, the path is a hyperbola and v∞ = √(2ε'). Escape from the circle still costs Δvesc = vc(√2 − 1). That increment is larger on the lower circle, because vc is larger. The higher circle was already closer to leaving.

A burn that escapes the low circle can leave more residual speed than the same Δv higher up. It does, once Δv is past the average of the two circular speeds. Below that, the higher burn can finish with a larger v∞, because it started higher in the well, even though it gained less energy. The readout prints both. A 6 km/s classroom burn from 400 km leaves 8.319 km/s at infinity. The same 6 km/s on the geosynchronous circle leaves 7.965 km/s. The Δv that only just escapes the 400 km circle, 3.178 km/s, leaves no excess there and 4.493 km/s from the higher circle.

The check

The same GM as the Escape desk

Earth’s GM is 0.39860 × 106 km3/s2. The volumetric mean radius is 6,371.000 km. Escape speed at that radius is 11.186 km/s, the figure the Earth Fact Sheet lists, and the figure the Escape desk prints at altitude 0. Add a classroom 400 km and r = 6,771 km. Circular speed is 7.673 km/s. Local escape is 10.851 km/s. The difference is 3.178 km/s, and that difference is vc(√2 − 1). The vis-viva desk prints the same 7.673 km/s and 10.851 km/s on its 400 km circle.

The geosynchronous radius uses that same GM and the sheet’s sidereal rotation period, 23.9345 hours: r³ = μ T² / (4π²). The radius is 42,164.190 km. The altitude above the volumetric radius is 35,793.190 km. A 1 km/s prograde burn gains 8.173 km²/s² at 400 km and 3.575 km²/s² on that circle. The cross terms are 7.673 km²/s² and 3.075 km²/s². The ½(Δv)² term is 0.500 km²/s² either way. A classroom circle 10,000 km up, r = 16,371 km, gains 5.434 km²/s² from the same 1 km/s. These altitudes are examples. The fact sheet does not print them.

Words

Oberth effect
The cross term in Δε = v·Δv + ½(Δv)². The same impulsive Δv changes orbital energy more when the craft is already moving faster. On a circle, faster means deeper in the well.
Specific energy
Energy per unit mass, ε = v²/2 − μ/r. Negative means bound. Zero is the parabola. Positive is a hyperbola, with v∞ = √(2ε). For a bound ellipse, ε = −μ/(2a).
Δv to escape
From a circular orbit, vesc − vc = vc(√2 − 1). It is larger in a lower orbit. The Escape desk is the two speeds. This desk is the burn that joins them, and what the same burn does higher up.
Hyperbolic excess
The speed left at infinity after an escape burn, v∞ = √(2ε'). A deeper burn gains more energy. It also starts from a deeper deficit. Which excess is larger depends on Δv. The readout keeps both.
Periapsis after the burn
A prograde burn from a circle makes the burn point the nearest point of the new orbit. Apoapsis, if the orbit is still bound, is on the far side. A retrograde burn is a different problem. This desk burns forward.
What the picture leaves out
Air, a finite burn, the planet’s bulge, other bodies, and a launch from the ground. The 400 km circle is the Earth Fact Sheet radius plus a classroom altitude, the same low orbit as the Hohmann and vis-viva desks. The 10,000 km circle is another classroom altitude. The geosynchronous circle is computed from GM and the 23.9345-hour sidereal day. It is not a satellite on a mission. The solar pair is two circles at printed semimajor axes, not the eccentric orbits those sheets describe. Not a trajectory to fly.

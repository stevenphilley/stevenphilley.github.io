Vol. Science · Astronomy · The balance point

Barycenter

Two bodies orbit a shared center of mass. The distance from body 1’s center to that point is r₁ = a · m₂ / (m₁ + m₂). The distance from body 2 is r₂ = a · m₁ / (m₁ + m₂). They add to a. The heavier body sits closer. Earth–Moon, from the Earth and Moon fact sheets, computes 4,670.8 km from Earth’s center, inside the volumetric mean radius of 6,371.000 km. Sun–Jupiter computes 742,428.6 km, 1.067 times the Sun’s volumetric mean radius of 695,700 km, just outside it. At two decimals that ratio is 1.07. Sun–Earth computes 449.3 km, inside the Sun. Pluto–Charon computes 2,126.4 km, outside Pluto’s 1,188 km radius. Alpha Centauri uses the masses and the angular axis on the binary-stars desk, and the barycenter falls far outside both stars. The transit and wobble desk is this same offset read as a speed. The Kepler desk is the year of one of these orbits when one mass is the Sun.

Classroom two-body picture, not an ephemeris. a on a fact-sheet preset is a semimajor axis. It is the distance only when the eccentricity is zero. Charon’s block prints 0.0. The other fact-sheet orbits do not. The drawn loop is a time-lapse of about 12 seconds, not the cited period. Disks and the balance point share one scale. A body smaller than a pixel is a point, and the inset has its own scale. The lower plot is an approximate schematic of the Sun about the solar-system barycenter. It is not a JPL Horizons barycenter.

What the number is

One balance point. The center of mass splits the separation in the inverse ratio of the masses. Body 1 carries the larger share of the mass when it sits closer to the point. Nothing here replaces a fact-sheet mass with a different printing. The Moon sheet’s Earth column is 5.9724 × 10^24 kg. This page uses the Earth sheet’s 5.9722 × 10^24 kg. They are not averaged. The Sun sheet prints a Sun/Earth mass ratio of 332,900. Dividing the Sun sheet’s 1,988,400 by the Earth sheet’s 5.9722 gives 332,942.63. The number field uses that division. The printed 332,900 is not substituted.

Inside, or outside. The comparison is r₁ against body 1’s radius. Earth–Moon is 4,670.8 km against 6,371.000 km, ratio 0.733, inside Earth. Sun–Jupiter is 742,428.6 km against 695,700 km, ratio 1.067, outside the Sun. The gap is 46,728.6 km. The Sun sheet’s photosphere thickness is ~500 km, and that thickness is not subtracted. Sun–Earth is 449.3 km, inside. Pluto–Charon is 2,126.4 km against 1,188 km, ratio 1.790, outside Pluto. The Lagrange desk uses the same three solar-system masses and separations and rounds the split to the nearest kilometer: 4,671 km, 449 km, and 742,429 km. The tenth stays on this page. They are not averaged.

The Sun’s wobble is the detection. Every planet pulls. The Sun moves so the center of mass stays put, and a radial-velocity search reads that motion as a Doppler shift. The transit desk computes the circular speed, K. This page computes the geometric offset. Jupiter alone, as a two-body problem, already puts the balance point just outside the Sun. The real solar system is not that one companion. The lower drawing adds the eight planets at the solar map’s approximate places. It is a schematic. It is not the two-body line, and it is not a Horizons barycenter.

What the words mean

Barycenter
The center of mass. For two bodies, r₁ = a m₂ / (m₁ + m₂) from body 1, and r₂ = a m₁ / (m₁ + m₂) from body 2. In one plane they stay on opposite sides of the point.
Inside or outside
r₁ compared with body 1’s radius. Inside means the balance point is buried in body 1, and body 1’s center circles a point under its own surface. Outside means both bodies orbit a point in the gap between them.
Semimajor axis
The size of the relative orbit, not the distance on a given day, unless the eccentricity is zero. Charon’s block prints 0.0. The Moon’s sheet prints 0.0549. Jupiter’s sheet prints 0.0487. Earth’s sheet prints 0.0167. Alpha Centauri’s table prints 0.51947.
Radial velocity
The part of the wobble aimed at the observer. The transit desk turns a circular orbit into K. This page stops at the length of the offset. A two-body Sun–Jupiter length is not the eight-planet length.
What this picture leaves out
Eccentricity in the drawn loop, the other moons of Pluto, Proxima, and every planet that is not the selected companion. The solar-system drawing puts those planets back, as an approximate sum, and still leaves out asteroids, comets, and a Horizons integration. The Lagrange desk’s nearest-kilometer lines are a rounding of three of these presets. They are not a second measurement.

PlayTurns both bodies about the barycenter. One loop is about 12 seconds. It is not the cited period. Press again to pause. With the drawing focused, Space does the same.
UnitsKilograms, Earth masses, or solar masses. Kilometers or astronomical units. The physical pair does not change when the unit changes. Earth masses divide by the Earth sheet. Solar masses divide by the Sun sheet.
m1, m2, a, R1, R2Logarithmic sliders, and the same values as numbers. Moving any of them leaves the pair button. With the drawing focused, Left and Right step the separation. Up and Down step mass 2. Shift takes a larger step.

An orbit that
does not close

A bound orbit is an ellipse. Eccentricity e is 0 for a circle and approaches 1 for a long ellipse that still comes back. At e = 1 the path is a parabola, the escape orbit. Above 1 it is a hyperbola: the Sun is still a focus, perihelion is still the near point, and the object leaves. The speed that remains at infinite distance is the hyperbolic excess speed, v∞. Vis-viva is v² = GM (2/r − 1/a). The signed axis is a = q / (1 − e), so at infinity v∞² = GM (e − 1) / q. GM is the Sun Fact Sheet value. The same formula, on a closed ellipse, is the vis-viva desk. How e stretches a bound orbit is the eccentricity desk. A comet that does come back is the comets desk. Three buttons load JPL Small-Body Database elements for 1I/‘Oumuamua, 2I/Borisov, and 3I/ATLAS. The excess speeds are computed here. JPL does not print v∞ as a field.

Educational schematic. One body and the Sun, drawn in the orbit plane. Eccentricity runs from a circle (e = 0) through a parabola (e = 1) to a hyperbola (e > 1). Perihelion stays on the right. For e < 1 the path closes and there is no v∞. At e = 1, v∞ is 0. For e > 1, v∞² = GM (e−1)/q, with the Sun Fact Sheet GM, 132,712 × 10⁶ km³/s², and q the perihelion distance. Bound, parabolic, unbound

Ellipse, e < 1
Bound ellipse. The path closes. Specific energy is negative, so there is no hyperbolic excess speed. The eccentricity desk stops short of e = 1. Halley’s JPL eccentricity on this page is 0.967936, still bound. The comets desk draws that ellipse.
Parabola, e = 1
Parabola. This is the escape orbit. v∞ is 0: it can reach infinite distance, with no speed left. It is the boundary between a captured orbit and one that escapes. The slider can sit on it.
Hyperbola, e > 1
Hyperbola. e > 1, so the path does not close. The object leaves, and the speed that remains at infinity is v∞. NASA Science says 3I/ATLAS does not follow a closed orbital path about the Sun. The same is true of any e above 1. The outbound excess speed matches the inbound one in this two-body picture.
Why they are fast
v∞ is the speed the Sun does not take away. Earth’s mean orbital velocity on the Earth Fact Sheet is 29.78 km/s. The three computed excess speeds, 26.405, 32.278, and 57.981 km/s, sit next to that number. NASA Science says an object like ‘Oumuamua is faint, and that survey telescopes such as Pan-STARRS1 are only recently able to catch one. 3I/ATLAS was reported on 1 July 2025, and NASA puts perihelion around 30 October 2025. The warning is months, not a decade of lead time.
A mission that waits
ESA’s Comet Interceptor is the mission built for a target that is not known yet. After launch it is parked, then it moves to intercept. ESA’s destination line is a pristine comet or an interstellar object. The launch period is late 2028 or early 2029. Three spacecraft fly the encounter, and two probes watch the comet from more than one direction at once. It is an ESA-led mission with JAXA. This page does not compute a launch window or a burn.

The table uses this JPL solution.
  • JPL Small-Body Database — 1P/Halley — orbit solution 75, the same elements as the comets desk. e = 0.9679359956953211, q = 0.5748638313743413 AU. Bound. The perihelion speed computes to 55.108 km/s, the same vis-viva result as that desk. There is no v∞.
  • NASA Science — ‘Oumuamua — discovered 19 October 2017 by Pan-STARRS1. No signs of cometary activity after perihelion on 9 September 2017, at 196,000 miles per hour (87.3 kilometers per second). Briefly an asteroid, then a slight acceleration, so it behaves more like a comet. The same page gives about 26.4 km/s (about 59,000 miles per hour) for the long cruise. This page’s v∞ from solution 16 is 26.405 km/s. The 87.3 km/s perihelion figure is NASA’s. Vis-viva here is 87.352 km/s.
  • NASA Science — Comet 2I/Borisov — first confirmed interstellar comet, discovered by Gennady Borisov on 30 August 2019. It developed a coma. Quoted there: “Whereas ‘Oumuamua appeared to be a rock, Borisov is really active, more like a normal comet.”
  • NASA Science — Comet 3I/ATLAS — ATLAS at Río Hurtado first reported the comet to the Minor Planet Center on 1 July 2025. Interstellar because the path is a hyperbola and does not close. Perihelion around 30 October 2025 at about 1.4 au. Hubble showed a teardrop-shaped cocoon of dust off a solid, icy nucleus. MAVEN showed the coma. A tail shows in PUNCH images.
  • ESA — Comet Interceptor — destination: pristine comet or interstellar object. Launch period late 2028 or early 2029. Parked after launch, then sent to intercept. Three spacecraft. ESA-led, with JAXA. This page does not add a trajectory of its own.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Sun Fact Sheet — GM 132,712 × 106 km3/s2. Volumetric mean radius 695,700 km. Every speed on this page uses that GM. A typed perihelion inside the Sun is rejected.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — the astronomical unit is exactly 149,597,870,700 m.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Earth Fact Sheet — mean orbital velocity 29.78 km/s. The comparison in the glossary is that printed speed. It is not recomputed here.
  • Vis-viva desk — v = √[μ (2/r − 1/a)] on a bound ellipse. This page is that formula at infinite r, where it becomes v∞.
  • Eccentricity desk — rp = a(1−e) for e below 1. This page keeps going, through the parabola, into the hyperbola.
  • Comets desk — Halley, Encke, 67P, and Hale–Bopp, all bound. The tails live there.
  • Asteroids desk — the main belt, which stays bound. 1I’s JPL class is Hyperbolic Asteroid. It is not a belt asteroid.
  • C3 desk — characteristic energy of a spacecraft leaving Earth, C3 = v∞². The symbol is the same. The v∞ on this page belongs to the interstellar object about the Sun, not to a departure burn.

The belt

Asteroids

Semi-major axis from 2.0 to 3.5 AU, and the Kirkwood gaps. Bound. 1I’s class is hyperbolic, not a main-belt orbit.

The stretch

Eccentricity

rp = a(1−e) and ra = a(1+e), for e below 1. Earth’s e is 0.0167. This slider crosses 1.

The speed

Vis-viva

v = √[μ (2/r − 1/a)] on a closed ellipse. The excess speed here is that equation at infinite distance.

The departure

C3

C3 = v∞² for a craft leaving Earth. Same symbol, a different orbit. Not a mission.

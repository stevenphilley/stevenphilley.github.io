Habitable zone
flux and temperature

Starlight thins with distance. At orbital distance d in AU, the flux relative to Earth’s is S/S⊕ = (L/L☉) / d². Watts per square metre use the NASA Earth Fact Sheet solar irradiance, 1361.0 W/m². Equilibrium temperature assumes that light is shared over the whole planet and radiated as a blackbody: Teq = [F (1−A) / (4σ)]¼, the same statement as T★ √(R★ / 2a) (1−A)¼. A is the Bond albedo. The strip is the habitable zone from Kopparapu et al. 2014, conservative and optimistic, for a 1 Earth-mass planet. Luminosity and temperature are the quantities on the blackbody desk and the HR diagram. Spectral type is the stars desk. The rough rings on exoplanets are not this calculation. Proxima is on nearest stars.

Classroom model. Full heat redistribution, emissivity 1, no atmosphere, no clouds, a circular orbit, and the star’s light only. A point inside the strip is not a habitable world, and a point outside is not a proof against liquid water under some other atmosphere. Mars is inside the conservative strip on this page. Venus is not. Earth’s no-greenhouse temperature is not its 288 K surface. The polynomial is for 2600 K to 7200 K and is not used outside that range.

Not a verdict

Inside the strip is a flux limit. It is not a living world.

The conservative edges are the runaway greenhouse and the maximum greenhouse. The optimistic edges are recent Venus and early Mars. Kopparapu et al. 2014 print those four for a 1 Earth-mass planet, and only for effective temperatures from 2600 K to 7200 K. The runaway-greenhouse row depends on mass. This page uses the 1 Earth-mass coefficients. Recent Venus, maximum greenhouse, and early Mars are the same at every mass in that table.

Mars sits inside the conservative strip and the fact sheet’s average temperature is about 214 K, under a thin atmosphere. Venus sits closer than the recent-Venus distance, and its average temperature is 737 K. Earth’s no-greenhouse result here is 255.1 K. The same fact sheet lists the average surface temperature as 288 K (15 °C). The gap is greenhouse warming, which this model leaves out.

Equilibrium temperature assumes the absorbed starlight is shared over the whole surface and radiated with emissivity 1. A planet that keeps the heat on the dayside runs hotter. Clouds, oceans, and a different gas mixture move the real edges. Being in the strip is not a detection of liquid water, and missing the strip is not a proof against it.

Literacy · the numbers on the readout

S / S⊕
Stellar flux at the planet divided by the flux at 1 AU from a star with L = L☉. The formula is (L/L☉) / d² with d in AU. It does not depend on albedo. Earth’s preset is 1.000 because the fact-sheet semimajor axis is 1.00000011 AU.
Watts per square metre
S/S⊕ times 1361.0 W/m², the solar irradiance on the NASA Earth Fact Sheet. Spreading the IAU nominal luminosity, 3.828 × 10²⁶ W, over a sphere of radius 1 au (149,597,870,700 m) gives 1361.17 W/m². This desk multiplies by the fact-sheet figure.
Equilibrium temperature
Teq = [F (1−A) / (4σ)]¼. The 4 is the whole surface emitting what a cross-section absorbs: full heat redistribution. σ is the CODATA Stefan–Boltzmann constant, the same computation as the blackbody desk. Emissivity is 1. There is no greenhouse.
The radius form
Teq = T★ √(R★ / 2a) (1−A)¼. R★ here is the radius implied by L = 4πR²σT⁴, using L = (L/L☉) × 3.828 × 10²⁶ W. That form and the flux form differ only because 1361.0 W/m² is not exactly the nominal luminosity spread over 1 au. At the Earth preset the gap is 0.008 K, inside the tenth this page prints.
Bond albedo
The fraction of incident starlight the planet reflects, over all wavelengths and all angles. Earth 0.294, Venus 0.77, Mars 0.250, from the NASA fact sheets. Geometric albedo is a different number and is not used here. Exoplanet Bond albedos are not measured on this page.
Greenhouse
An atmosphere can let starlight in and hold infrared on the way out. Earth’s surface is warmer than Teq. Venus is much warmer. This desk does not add that term. The fact-sheet surface temperatures are printed beside the model so the gap stays visible.
The four limits
Optimistic inner edge: recent Venus. Conservative inner edge: runaway greenhouse. Conservative outer edge: maximum greenhouse. Optimistic outer edge: early Mars. Seff = Seff☉ + aT★ + bT★² + cT★³ + dT★⁴, with T★ = Teff − 5780 K. Distance is √((L/L☉) / Seff) AU. The zero of the polynomial is 5780 K, the solar temperature in that paper, not the IAU nominal 5772 K.
Outside 2600–7200 K
Kopparapu et al. 2014 state the coefficients for 2600 K ≤ Teff ≤ 7200 K. Below or above that, this page still computes flux and Teq, and it does not draw a zone. TRAPPIST-1’s 2566 K is the preset that shows the refusal. The endpoints 2600 K and 7200 K are inside the window.

Sources · fact sheets, IAU, CODATA, and the papers

Cited, not invented

  • NASA NSSDCA — Earth Fact Sheet — Bond albedo 0.294, solar irradiance 1361.0 W/m², black-body temperature 254.0 K, average temperature 288 K (15 °C), J2000 semimajor axis 1.00000011 AU. This desk’s formula with 1361.0 and 0.294 returns 255.1 K, not the sheet’s 254.0 K.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Venus Fact Sheet — Bond albedo 0.77, solar irradiance 2601.3 W/m², black-body temperature 226.6 K, average temperature 737 K, J2000 semimajor axis 0.72333199 AU.
  • NASA NSSDCA — Mars Fact Sheet — Bond albedo 0.250, solar irradiance 586.2 W/m², black-body temperature 209.8 K, average temperature ~214 K, J2000 semimajor axis 1.52366231 AU.
  • IAU 2015 Resolution B3 (Prša et al. 2016, AJ 152, 41) — nominal solar luminosity 3.828 × 10²⁶ W, nominal solar effective temperature 5772 K, nominal solar radius 6.957 × 10⁸ m. Conversion constants, not a measurement of the Sun. The resolution text is also at the IAU archive.
  • IAU 2012 Resolution B2 — 1 au = 149,597,870,700 m exactly. Used when the radius form of Teq is compared with the flux form.
  • NIST CODATA — Stefan–Boltzmann constant — σ = 5.670374419… × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, exact, from the defined h, c, and k. This desk computes σ = 2π⁵k⁴ / (15h³c²) using h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 299,792,458 m/s, and k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K.
  • Kopparapu et al. 2014, ApJL 787, L29 — habitable zones for 2600 K to 7200 K. Equation (4): Seff = Seff☉ + aT★ + bT★² + cT★³ + dT★⁴, T★ = Teff − 5780 K. Equation (5): d = √((L/L☉) / Seff) AU. Table 1, 1 M⊕ row, as printed: recent Venus 1.776, 2.136×10⁻⁴, 2.533×10⁻⁸, −1.332×10⁻¹¹, −3.097×10⁻¹⁵; runaway greenhouse 1.107, 1.332×10⁻⁴, 1.58×10⁻⁸, −8.308×10⁻¹², −1.931×10⁻¹⁵; maximum greenhouse 0.356, 6.171×10⁻⁵, 1.698×10⁻⁹, −3.198×10⁻¹², −5.575×10⁻¹⁶; early Mars 0.32, 5.547×10⁻⁵, 1.526×10⁻⁹, −2.874×10⁻¹², −5.011×10⁻¹⁶. The 2013 paper is Kopparapu et al. 2013, ApJ 765, 131.
  • Ribas et al. 2017, A&A 603, A58 — Proxima Centauri: Teff = 2980 ± 80 K, L = 0.00151 ± 0.00008 L☉. The same central values as the blackbody desk.
  • Anglada-Escudé et al. 2016, Nature 536, 437 — Proxima b: semimajor axis 0.0485 AU, credible interval 0.0434–0.0526 AU. That paper’s star was Teff = 3050 K and L = 0.00155 L☉. This preset keeps the later stellar parameters and the discovery orbit.
  • Agol et al. 2021, PSJ 2, 1 — TRAPPIST-1: Teff = 2566 ± 26 K, planet e semimajor axis 0.02925 ± 0.00025 AU. Luminosity (5.53 ± 0.19) × 10⁻⁴ L☉ adopted from Ducrot et al. 2020. 2566 K is below 2600 K, so the zone is not drawn.
  • Jenkins et al. 2015, AJ 150, 56 — Kepler-452b: Teff = 5757 ± 85 K, semimajor axis 1.046 AU (+0.019, −0.015), insolation 1.10 S⊕ (+0.29, −0.22), equilibrium temperature 265 K (+15, −13) assuming Bond albedo 0.3 and complete redistribution. L/L☉ on this preset is 1.10 × (1.046)². They compared the planet with Kopparapu et al. 2013 and called it slightly outside the conservative zone.

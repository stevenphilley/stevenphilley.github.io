Atmosphere

A clickable cross-section of Earth’s air — five named layers, approximate heights, and what each region does for weather, life, and orbit. Pair with the science status desk, live weather, and the clean-air / natural-life themes on Power.

What the environment needs

Most of the mass of the atmosphere sits in the troposphere — the air we breathe and the climate we live in. Stable climate, clean air (low pollution, managed CO2 and particulates), and a recovering ozone shield in the stratosphere are the practical stakes. Higher layers matter for meteors, aurora, satellites, and space weather, but the living world depends on the lower stack staying healthy.

Layer 01

Troposphere

~0–12 km · ~0–7.5 mi (tropopause ~8–18 km by latitude)

Temperature trend

Cools with altitude — roughly −6.5 °C per km in the standard atmosphere, until the tropopause.

What happens here

Weather, clouds, most of Earth’s air mass (~75–80%), the water cycle, and nearly all human activity.

Environmental needs / stakes

Clean air, a stable climate, and a working water cycle. Pollution, excess CO2, and particulates hit this layer first — and everything that breathes.

Useful reference altitudes

  • Sea level — 0 km · baseline for pressure and standard atmosphere tables.
  • Typical commercial flight — ~10–12 km (~33,000–39,000 ft), still inside the troposphere / near the tropopause.
  • Ozone layer (peak density) — roughly 15–35 km in the lower–middle stratosphere (varies).
  • International Space Station — ~400 km average, in the thermosphere.
  • Kármán line (convention) — 100 km, often cited as the edge of space (still within the thermosphere in this scheme).

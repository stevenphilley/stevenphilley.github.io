Cosmology desk

Astronomy grows a cosmology page. A redshift becomes an age, a lookback time, and three distances, from a numerical integral of Hogg’s E(z) with no radiation term. Planck 2018 Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing: H0 = 67.36 ± 0.54, Ωm = 0.3153 ± 0.0073, ΩΛ = 0.6847 ± 0.0073. The published age for that column is 13.797 ± 0.023 Gyr, radiation included. This integral leaves radiation out and gives 13.801 Gyr. At z = 1: lookback 7.954 Gyr, age then 5.847 Gyr, comoving 3,402.7 Mpc (11.10 Gly), luminosity 6,805.4 Mpc (22.20 Gly), angular diameter 1,701.3 Mpc (5.55 Gly), 8.248 kpc per arcsec. Angular-diameter distance peaks at z = 1.588. SH0ES (Riess et al. 2022) H0 = 73.04 ± 1.04 keeps the Planck densities, because that paper does not publish Ωm, and the age is 12.728 Gyr. Cited objects: Coma, Colless and Dunn 1996, mean cz = 6853 km/s so z = cz/c; 3C 273, SIMBAD z = 0.15756751; GN-z11, Bunker et al. 2023, z = 10.6034; CMB last scattering, Planck z* = 1089.92. The chart runs to z = 20, so the CMB marker is off the chart. r* = 144.43 Mpc is the sound horizon, not the distance to the CMB. Flat is the default. Curvature uses Hogg’s transverse distance. The redshift desk is the shift. The distance ladder stops at cz/H0. Not a fit and not a catalog.

Distance ladder desk

Astronomy grows a distance-ladder page. The modulus is m − M = 5 log10(d / 10 pc) + A. A = 0 is the magnitude desk. m = 10, M = 0, A = 0 gives 1000 pc. A = 1 gives 10 × 10^1.8 pc, about 630.96 pc. The Sun at 10 pc stays at +4.83, and 10 pc is 32.616 ly. Parallax is d(pc) = 1/p(″). The fractional error σ_d/d equals σ_p/p, and the bounds 1/(p±σ) are asymmetric. Sirius is ESA’s worked example, p = 0.37921″, 1/p = 2.637 pc, with no uncertainty on that page. δ Cephei parallax is Benedict et al. 2007 Table 11: 3.66 ± 0.15 mas, so 1000/3.66 pc, near 1000/3.81 pc, far 1000/3.51 pc. Table 11’s (m−M)0 = 7.19 includes an LKH correction of −0.01 mag. Plain inversion is not that modulus, and this page does not apply LKH. The Cepheid line is the same paper, Table 12 column B07, Johnson V: M_V = −4.05 ± 0.02 + (−2.43 ± 0.12)(log10 P − 1). P = 10 d gives −4.05. δ Cephei Table 2 is P = 5.36627 d, ⟨V⟩ = 3.960, A_V = 0.23. The line gives M_V about −3.393. Table 11’s own M_V is −3.47 ± 0.11. The V residual scatter is 0.10 mag, a distance factor 10^0.02, shown as an illustration and not an error bar. Type Ia uses Richardson et al. 2014 Table 1, bias-corrected normal mean M_B = −19.25 ± 0.20, standard deviation 0.50 mag, N = 239. Hillebrandt and Niemeyer’s −19.30 is the H0 = 60 figure and is not the candle. SN 2011fe (Richmond and Smith 2012, Table 5): B max = 10.00 ± 0.02, A_B = 0.11 ± 0.03, so μ = 29.14 and the distance is about 6.730 Mpc. They adopt 29.10 ± 0.15 for M101, about 6.607 Mpc. The two are not forced to agree. Hubble flow is d = cz/H0. Coma (Colless and Dunn 1996) has mean cz = 6853 km/s and σ = 1082 km/s. Planck 67.4 gives about 101.68 Mpc. SH0ES 73.04 gives about 93.83 Mpc. The dispersion scale is larger than that gap. The bars are a sketch: Proxima’s 1.3 pc to 100 pc (NASA’s “a few hundred light-years”; NASA does not print 100 pc), Cepheids to ∼40 Mpc, Hubble flow ∼100–600 Mpc (Scolnic and Vincenzi). Not a catalog.

Black hole desk

Astronomy grows a black-hole page for a non-rotating Schwarzschild hole. Mass runs from 1 to 10 billion solar masses on a log slider. r_s = 2GM/c². The photon sphere is 1.5 r_s. The ISCO is 3 r_s. The IAU 2015 nominal solar mass parameter, GM☉ = 1.3271244 × 10^20 m³ s^−2, and c = 299792458 m/s give r_s = 2.953 km. A hovering observer from 1.2 to 30 r_s reads √(1 − r_s/r). At 10 r_s that factor is 0.9487. The horizon tide across a 2 m body is 2GM L/r³, about 2.061×10^10 m/s² at one solar mass. Hawking temperature is ħ c³ / (8 π k GM), 6.170×10^−8 K at one solar mass. The evaporation time is the horizon-area estimate t = 5120 π G² M³ / (ħ c⁴), 2.095×10^67 Julian years at one solar mass, labeled an order of magnitude. Mean density uses the 2022 CODATA G, 6.67430 × 10^−11. Presets: 1 and 10 solar masses, computed; Cygnus X-1, 21.2 ± 2.2 M☉ (Miller-Jones et al. 2021); Sagittarius A*, (4.297 ± 0.012) × 10^6 M☉ statistical, systematics about 40,000 M☉ (GRAVITY Collaboration 2022, A&A 657, L12); M87*, 6.5 ± 0.2 (stat) ± 0.7 (sys) × 10^9 M☉ (EHT 2019, ApJL 875, L6). Real holes spin. Bardeen, Press, and Teukolsky 1972 move the photon orbit and the ISCO with that spin. This page holds them at 1.5 r_s and 3 r_s.

Stellar lifetime desk

Astronomy grows a stellar-lifetime page. Mass runs from 0.1 to 60 solar masses on a log slider. Inside 0.179 < M ≤ 31, luminosity is the six-piece fit of Eker et al. 2018, Table 4: log10(L/L☉) = a log10(M/M☉) + b. The pieces are separate lines. They are not joined, and they are not used outside that window. At 1 M☉ the low-mass line gives log L = −0.007, about 0.984 L☉, not 1. Main-sequence lifetime is t ≈ 10 Gyr × M/L. With the IAU nominal L = 1 the Sun is exactly 10 Gyr. With the fit it is about 10.16 Gyr. The 10 Gyr zero is the classroom round number beside NASA’s 4.6-billion-year-old Sun plus about 5 billion years still on the main sequence (sum 9.6). It is not a model age. End-state labels follow NASA Science: under 8 M☉ a white dwarf, about 8 to 20 a neutron star, heavier a black hole. Heger et al. 2003 move those edges with metallicity, rotation, and mass loss. The plot marks the Planck 2018 age from Table 2, column TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing: 13.797 ± 0.023 Gyr. The +BAO column is 13.787 ± 0.020 Gyr. Cited presets: Proxima Centauri 0.120 ± 0.003 M☉ and 0.00151 ± 0.00008 L☉ (Ribas et al. 2017; below the fit; 10 × 0.120 / 0.00151 is about 795 Gyr), Sirius A 2.063 ± 0.023 M☉ and 24.74 ± 0.70 L☉ (Bond et al. 2017; about 834 Myr; YREC age 237 ± 15 Myr), Vega mass 2.15 M☉ with +0.10 and −0.15 (Monnier et al. 2012) and 44 ± 2 L☉ (Hill, Gulliver, and Adelman 2010; about 489 Myr, shorter than the cited 700 Myr age), Rigel log(L/L☉) = 5.08 and a track mass of 23 M☉ (Moravveji et al. 2012). Rigel is a blue supergiant, so the fit at 23 M☉ is not its present luminosity. Formula outputs are approximate scaling estimates. Not a stellar-evolution model.

Telescope optics desk

Astronomy grows a telescope-optics page. Focal ratio is focal length over aperture. Magnification is telescope focal length over eyepiece focal length, and a Barlow multiplies that effective focal length. Exit pupil is aperture over magnification. The rough true field is apparent field divided by magnification. Light grasp compares the aperture with a 7 mm pupil, the traditional maximum Sky & Telescope states. Rayleigh’s criterion is 1.22 λ/D. Dawes’s empirical limit is printed as 116/D in millimetres and as 4.56/D in inches. A classroom 200 mm f/6 with a 25 mm eyepiece and a 50° apparent field is 48×, exit pupil 4.17 mm, true field 1.042° (62.5′), light grasp 816×, Rayleigh 0.692″ at 550 nm, and Dawes 0.580″ (116/D) and 0.579″ (4.56/D). At naked-eye magnitude 6.0 the collecting-area estimate is 13.28. Bowen’s 1947 formula gives 12.94. Hubble uses the STScI primer’s 2.4 m aperture at f/24, focal length 57.6 m. JWST uses NASA’s 6.5 m and 131.4 m, which compute to f/20.22. The 7×50, 80 mm, and 200 mm sets are round classroom numbers, not catalog models. Not a buying guide.

Habitable zone desk

Astronomy grows a habitable-zone page: stellar flux S/S⊕ = (L/L☉)/d², and watts per square metre from the NASA Earth Fact Sheet solar irradiance, 1361.0 W/m². Equilibrium temperature assumes full heat redistribution and emissivity 1: T_eq = [F(1−A)/(4σ)]¼. Earth’s Bond albedo 0.294 at 1 AU gives S/S⊕ = 1.000 and T_eq = 255.1 K. The fact sheet’s average surface temperature is 288 K. The strip is Kopparapu et al. 2014, conservative and optimistic, 1 Earth mass, valid from 2600 K to 7200 K and not drawn outside that range. Presets: Venus, Earth, and Mars from the NASA fact sheets; Proxima Centauri b (Ribas et al. 2017; Anglada-Escudé et al. 2016); TRAPPIST-1 e (Agol et al. 2021), whose 2566 K host is below the polynomial; Kepler-452b (Jenkins et al. 2015). A point in the strip is not a habitability verdict.

Redshift desk

Astronomy grows a redshift page: z = (λ_obs − λ_rest)/λ_rest. At z = 1 the special-relativistic Doppler formula gives β = 0.6, while c·z equals the speed of light and is not a speed. Cosmological redshift is 1+z = a_now/a_then. The nearby Hubble toy is v = H0·d, with Planck 2018 H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc and SH0ES 2022 H0 = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc. At a classroom 10 Mpc those central values are 674 km/s and 730.4 km/s. Cited presets: Andromeda z = −0.001 (SIMBAD), NGC 4258 z = 0.00158 (BASS DR2), 3C 273 z = 0.15756751 (SIMBAD), GN-z11 z = 10.6034 (Bunker et al. 2023). Rest wavelengths are NIST: Lyα 121.567 nm vacuum, Ca II K 393.36614 nm air, Ca II H 396.84673 nm air, Hβ 486.132 nm air, Hα 656.280 nm air. The visible shade is the NASA Imagine classroom window, 400–700 nm. Not a catalog spectrum and not a distance to those objects.

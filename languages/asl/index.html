ASL trainer

Fingerspelling, numbers, and a short vocabulary, drawn as original diagrams. Practice with Leitner flashcards, two quizzes, a fingerspelling reader, and a spell-it drill. Progress stays in this browser. You can save it to a text file and load it back.

ASL is a language. It has its own grammar. It is not a code for English words, and it is not mime. It belongs to Deaf communities. This page is a beginner aid: simplified drawings, not a class, and not a substitute for Deaf instructors. Facial expression and movement carry meaning a still picture cannot show. Check a video before you rely on a sign.

      Modes

      Modes
      LearnAlphabet, numbers 0–20, and vocabulary by category. The large picture sits with handshape, location, movement, and palm. Previous and Next, or the grid.
      FlashcardsLeitner boxes. Show the answer, then Know or Don't know. Know moves the card up a box. Don't know sends it back to box 1. Due now is a round of up to 20 cards.
      RecognizeSee a handshape. Pick the letter, number, or word. Keys 1–4.
      RecallSee the letter or word. Pick the handshape. Keys 1–4.
      ReaderA word is spelled one letter at a time. Set the seconds per letter, press Play, then type the word. J and Z include a motion arrow.
      Spell itThe target word stays on screen. For each highlighted letter, pick the handshape.
      ProgressStreak, accuracy, and box counts. Save downloads a text file. Load reads that file back. Reset asks first. Nothing is uploaded.

      How to read a diagram

      Palm toward you, the signer's right hand, thumb on the right of the drawing, unless the text says the palm faces in, down, or to the side. A dot on the small figure is the location: forehead, chin, chest, and so on. Arrows and numbered frames stand in for movement. If your system asks for less motion, the arrows stay still.

      The same handshape can be a letter and a number. V and 2, O and 0, F and 9. The label says which set you are in. The quizzes do not mix those sets.

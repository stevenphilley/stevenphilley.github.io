Nine
Chakras

नव चक्र

Chakras are the points where the subtle body's channels cross — nine stations on a single vertical line running from the base of the spine to just above the head. Most modern accounts stop at seven. This one includes the two the tantric texts describe and the popular version leaves out: Anandakanda, the lotus beneath the heart, and Bindu Visarga at the back of the skull.

  1. Muladhara
  2. Svadhisthana
  3. Manipura
  4. Anandakanda
  5. Anahata
  6. Vishuddha
  7. Ajna
  8. Bindu Visarga
  9. Sahasrara

The order below is ascending, which is how the classical sources describe it — the column is climbed, not surveyed. Each entry lists the Sanskrit name and what it literally means, where the chakra sits, its element and seed sound, and the number of petals traditionally assigned to its lotus. The petal counts are drawn to scale in the mark beside each name.

Seed sounds and petal counts follow the Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa and related tantric material. Color associations are largely a twentieth-century addition and vary by source; they're used here as a wayfinding device rather than a claim.

01

Muladhara

मूलाधार

"root support"

Location
Base of the spine
Element
Earth · pṛthvī
Petals
4
Seed sound
लं · LAM
Governs
Ground, shelter, survival

The chakra of having a floor under you. Its concerns are the plainest ones — food, shelter, safety, belonging to a place — and the texts put the coiled kundalini here, asleep, before anything else in the system begins. Nothing above it works well if this one is unattended.

  • stability
  • survival
  • belonging
  • patience
  • physical vitality

In balance

Steady and unhurried. At home in your own body, able to handle practical things without them becoming emergencies.

Out of balance

Persistent anxiety about money or safety. Restlessness, or its opposite — inertia and a sense of being unwelcome anywhere.

02

Svadhisthana

स्वाधिष्ठान

"one's own dwelling place"

Location
Below the navel, at the sacrum
Element
Water · apas
Petals
6
Seed sound
वं · VAM
Governs
Appetite, pleasure, making

Water is the element that moves, pools, and takes the shape of whatever holds it — which is a fair description of desire. This is the seat of appetite in the widest sense: pleasure, sexuality, and the raw willingness to bring something into existence that wasn't there before.

  • creativity
  • desire
  • fluidity
  • pleasure
  • emotional range

In balance

Feelings move through rather than lodge. Enjoyment doesn't require a justification, and work you care about starts without a fight.

Out of balance

Numbness or compulsion, which are the same failure from opposite ends. Guilt attached to pleasure; creative work that stalls at the first line.

03

Manipura

मणिपूर

"city of jewels"

Location
At the navel
Element
Fire · agni
Petals
10
Seed sound
रं · RAM
Governs
Will, heat, transformation

Where food becomes energy and intention becomes action — digestion and willpower treated as one process, because both are questions of what you can metabolize. This is the chakra of heat, and heat has two readings: what it cooks, and what it burns.

  • will
  • discipline
  • confidence
  • transformation
  • autonomy

In balance

You decide, then follow through. Anger arrives as information about a boundary rather than as an eruption.

Out of balance

Passivity, or its mirror image in domination. Chronic indecision, shame, and the particular exhaustion of burning without cooking anything.

04

Anandakanda

अनन्दकन्द

"root of bliss" · also called Hrit, हृत्

Location
Just below Anahata
Element
Solar · beyond the five
Petals
8
Seed sound
— none fixed
Governs
Devotion, longing, refuge

The eight-petaled lotus the seven-chakra summaries drop. It isn't about love directed outward — that's the chakra above it — but about longing and orientation. The kalpavṛkṣa, the wish-fulfilling tree, is said to grow here, and this is where a practitioner seats their chosen deity. The chamber where devotion stays private.

  • devotion
  • longing
  • inner refuge
  • gratitude
  • imagination

In balance

A sense of being pointed at something. Wanting is specific, and having somewhere to put it is itself a kind of rest.

Out of balance

Dryness. Cynicism as a defense against caring. Wanting a great many things and none of them all the way through.

05

Anahata

अनाहत

"unstruck"

Location
Center of the chest
Element
Air · vāyu
Petals
12
Seed sound
यं · YAM
Governs
Compassion, balance, contact

Named for a sound produced without two things striking together — the one noise in the world that isn't caused. It sits at the hinge of the column, where the body's concerns below meet the mind's above, which is why it gets described as the place where the two halves of a person either meet or don't.

  • compassion
  • love
  • equilibrium
  • forgiveness
  • connection

In balance

You can be close to someone without disappearing into them. Grief and affection both move at their own speed.

Out of balance

Defensiveness, or over-giving as a way of staying in control. Isolation described to yourself as independence.

06

Vishuddha

विशुद्ध

"purified"

Location
The throat
Element
Ether · ākāśa
Petals
16
Seed sound
हं · HAM
Governs
Speech, truth, discernment

Its element is ether — the subtlest of the five, and the one that holds the other four. The obvious subject is speech, but the real subject is everything upstream of it: what you've decided is true, and what you're prepared to name out loud.

  • expression
  • truth
  • discernment
  • listening
  • resonance

In balance

You say the thing. You can also stay quiet without it costing you anything.

Out of balance

The throat tightening ahead of a hard conversation. Circling a subject for an hour. A steady gap between what you think and what comes out.

07

Ajna

आज्ञा

"command"

Location
Between the brows
Element
Mind · beyond the five
Petals
2
Seed sound
ॐ · OM
Governs
Insight, perception, clarity

Two petals, one for each of the side channels — iḍā and piṅgalā — which terminate here after winding the length of the spine. It's called the command center because this is where the split between the one looking and the thing looked at starts to close.

  • insight
  • intuition
  • clarity
  • perspective
  • discrimination

In balance

You see the shape of a thing and not only its parts. Intuition shows up quietly and turns out to have been right.

Out of balance

Rumination mistaken for thinking. Rigidity at one extreme; at the other, no filter at all and belief in whatever arrived last.

08

Bindu Visarga

बिन्दु विसर्ग

"the falling drop"

Location
Back of the head, at the crown
Element
Lunar · nectar
Petals
1 point, crescent
Seed sound
ः · visarga
Governs
Renewal, coolness, reserve

The second of the two the seven-chakra model omits. Hatha texts place amṛta — a nectar — here, dripping downward to be consumed by the fire at the navel; several well-known practices exist for no reason other than to slow that loss. Its association is lunar, and everything lunar in this system means the same thing: coolness, subtlety, and not spending what you have.

  • subtlety
  • renewal
  • stillness
  • conservation
  • receptivity

In balance

Recovery actually works. There's a reserve you haven't drawn down, and you can feel that there is.

Out of balance

Depletion that sleep doesn't touch. A mind running hot with nothing left to cool it.

09

Sahasrara

सहस्रार

"thousand-petaled"

Location
At or above the crown
Element
None
Petals
1,000
Seed sound
silence
Governs
Union, release, whole awareness

Several of the sources say plainly that this is not a chakra. The other eight are stations inside the body; this is what remains when the climb is finished. It has no element and no seed syllable — the texts reach for a thousand petals and silence, which is a way of saying the vocabulary has run out.

  • union
  • transcendence
  • awareness
  • release
  • wholeness

In balance

Proportion. Your own life sits inside something larger without that needing to be argued for.

Out of balance

Grandiosity at one end. At the other, a flat and disenchanted world in which nothing means much.

On counting to nine

The seven-chakra list most people know comes from a single sixteenth-century Sanskrit text, the Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa, as filtered through its 1918 English translation. It was never the only scheme. Different lineages count six, seven, nine, twelve, or more, and the count reflects what a given practice needs to talk about rather than a disagreement about anatomy.

The nine here are the classical seven plus two that appear in the same body of literature and get quietly dropped in summary. There's a second nine-chakra arrangement in circulation — the seven plus an Earth Star below the feet and a Soul Star above the head — which comes out of late twentieth-century energy-healing practice rather than the Sanskrit sources. Both are defensible; they're answering different questions.

  • Petal counts, seed sounds, and elements follow the tantric texts.
  • Rainbow color mapping dates to the 1970s and is a modern overlay.
  • Nothing here is medical. It's a map of attention, and it's useful in the way maps are useful.

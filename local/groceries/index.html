What's hot in groceries

Mid-September in the Bay Area is the delicious in-between: summer tomatoes still swaggering, fall apples sneaking onto the table. Seasonal look-fors for Campbell carts — not scraped circulars, not invented dollar signs.

Peak produce now

California's shoulder season — grab the last great summer fruit before it quietly exits stage left.

  • Heirloom & slicing tomatoes

    Late August–September is peak flavor on the Central Coast / Bay Area — sun-warmed, irregular, worth the juice on your cutting board.

  • Sweet & frying peppers

    Bell, lunchbox, Jimmy Nardello, shishito — September is peak pepper season; blister a panful with salt and call it dinner.

  • Table grapes

    California grape harvest hits hard in September; crisp clusters for snacks, cheese boards, or that picnic-blanket flex.

  • Figs

    Late-summer into fall window — soft, jammy, and gone before you remember to buy more.

  • Early apples & pears

    Fall's advance team. Pair with honey if you're leaning into the sweet-new-year energy of September.

  • Melons (last call)

    Still hanging around farmers markets and produce aisles — buy ripe, eat soon, mourn later.

  • Eggplant & summer squash

    Still abundant; roast, grill, or turn into one more ratatouille before the oven becomes soup season.

  • Sweet corn

    Late-summer ears are still in play — eat same-day if you can; sugar doesn't wait politely.

Pantry / seasonal staples

Stock the shelf while the season is loud — future-you will thank present-you in November.

  • California almonds & walnuts

    Harvest season ramps through fall; good moment to refresh the nut jar (yes, actual nuts — not the Campbell kind).

  • Extra tomatoes for sauce / freezer

    When peak heirlooms are cheap-looking and fragrant, buy a second batch and freeze, roast, or can for winter pasta nights.

  • Local honey

    September sweet-tooth tradition energy — drizzle on apples, yogurt, or toast without overthinking it.

Local shopping notes

Campbell / South Bay angles — evergreen pointers, not inventory claims.

Downtown Campbell Farmers' Market

Sundays, year-round, roughly 9 am–1 pm on E. Campbell Ave (Urban Village FMA). Peak late-summer piles: tomatoes, peppers, stone fruit leftovers, early apples. Confirm festival blackout weekends on the Downtown Campbell calendar if you're planning around Boogie / Oktoberfest.

Everyday carts

Safeway covers delivery for the Union Ave / 95008 orbit; Trader Joe's Campbell sits on Bascom for quick specialty runs; Costco Cupertino is the bulk-pantry gravity well. Treat weekly ads as ephemeral — this page stays seasonal, not price-scraped.

Nearby markets

Los Gatos CFM and Cupertino / De Anza markets are easy Sunday hops if Campbell is packed. Same California produce calendar, slightly different parking drama.

Quick tips

  • Smell tomatoes at the stem end — perfume beats perfect shape every time.
  • Buy peppers firm and glossy; wrinkly ones are for roasting today, not lunch Tuesday.
  • Grapes last longer if you don't wash until serving (fridge humidity is already doing enough).
  • Figs are a same-day or next-day fruit — treat them like soft cheese with a clock.
  • If you're meal-prepping for heat: cold tomato salads, blistered peppers, and melon for dessert beat another oven session.

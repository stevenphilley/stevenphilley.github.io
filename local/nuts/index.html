Feb–Mar 2026 · The Garret / City Council

Vice mayor restaurant incident

Public reporting places the episode at The Garret bar and restaurant near The Pruneyard (Feb 2, 2026), involving then–Vice Mayor Elliot Scozzola. Per an employee account in San José Spotlight: alleged intense staring / leering until the person asked him to stop, then a napkin apology. Campbell police said they reviewed security video showing a long stay and drinking. On March 3 the council stripped the vice mayor title; on March 24 it appointed Councilmember Anne Bybee. A pattern of earlier police contacts was already in public reporting — see the Spotlight piece rather than a second retelling here.

Ongoing · Hyde Park / downtown

Water tower light rave

The downtown Campbell water tower (1st St / Orchard City Dr, Hyde Park landmark) lights up — sometimes in colors for occasions — and the town has a soft obsession when the night light flickers, cycles, or misbehaves. People notice. People talk. People treat it like civic theater. Framed here as neighborhood chatter, not a news cite (no solid news hit found for a “rave”).

Oct 1989 · Hyde Park water tower

Water tower vs Loma Prieta

Fun fact from a Campbell Museums cannery tour: former councilmember Mike Kotowski recalled driving past the downtown water tower during the Loma Prieta quake, looking up, seeing it sway, and thinking — roughly — “If there’s anything going down, it’s that thing.” It stood. Pair with the flickering-light lore card; this one’s the earthquake cameo, not the night-mode gossip.

~Sep/Oct 2024 → smashed Aug 2026 · Hwy 17 / Camden Ave

The silver ant on 17

After Burning Man, an unofficial ~5-ft silver ant sculpture appeared on the hillside along northbound Hwy 17 near the Camden Ave exit — never city-sanctioned (Council Policy 1.30 art approval), somehow beloved anyway. Commuters photographed it; someone gave it a Christmas hat and lights. Vandals smashed it around August 2026. Creator unknown; vandals unknown. Brief, bright mascot career.

1990 · Hamilton/Bascom → 300 Grant St

The house that moved

The Ainsley House — a 1925 Tudor Cotswold / Arts & Crafts cottage built for canning pioneer John Colpitts Ainsley — was donated to the City and physically relocated from the Hamilton/Bascom orchard corner into downtown at 300 Grant St. Now a museum (NRHP 2005). Quirk: Campbell literally slid a canning pioneer’s cottage into the civic heart of town.

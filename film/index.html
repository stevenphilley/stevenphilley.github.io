Getting started

Pipeline · California support · copyright basics

Filmmaking is a pipeline, not just a camera: development, pre-production (planning, locations, permits, insurance), production, post (edit, sound, color, clearances), then distribution or exhibition. Beginners often under-weight pre-pro, sound, and clearances.

California’s public support network

The California Film Commission (CFC) is a state agency (GO-Biz) for state-property permits, location resources, troubleshooting, and liaison to regional film partners statewide. City and county offices handle most street, park, and civic filming. Bay Area shoots need the correct jurisdiction — not one statewide “film permit.”

Motion pictures & U.S. copyright

Copyright in a motion picture attaches when the work is created and fixed. Registration is optional for protection to arise, but is generally required before an infringement suit and unlocks certain remedies. Indie filmmakers often confuse “I shot it so I own it” with festival-ready chain of title, music, clips, and talent releases.

  • Circular 45 — Copyright Registration for Motion Pictures (PDF)
  • Copyright Office Fair Use IndexFour-factor doctrine; not a blanket short-clip rule

Permits & offices

FilmLA ≠ CFC · San Jose · Campbell · County Parks · Film SF · East Bay · Peninsula

Do not conflate FilmLA and CFC. FilmLA coordinates permitting for Los Angeles-area jurisdictions; it does not approve permits itself, and it does not cover the Bay Area. CFC is the state agency and issues permits only for state-owned/operated property. Campbell / San Jose / Santa Clara County filming is not a FilmLA path.

CFC — state property permits

Apply via CFC for State Parks/beaches, Caltrans highways and facilities, and other state agency buildings. CFC publishes lead-time guidance on its permits pages (including longer windows for drones and Caltrans closures). CFC does not charge a permit-processing fee; State Parks or Caltrans may charge review or monitor fees — confirm on the live pages. Highway, state park, or state-building shoots need CFC, not the City of Campbell.

Rights · locations · insurance

Jurisdiction · CFC & San Jose COI · fair use · FAA Part 107

Public property almost always needs a permit from the owning agency. Private property needs landowner permission (location agreement). Skipping permits is a common indie failure mode — shut downs, insurance voids, and festival or distribution problems.

CFC insurance minimums (state property)

As published by CFC for state property: general liability typically ≥ $1,000,000 per occurrence with additional-insured endorsement naming “the State of California, including its officers, agents and employees”; Workers’ Comp (or exemption waiver if no employees); auto liability ≥ $1,000,000 when autos are used; UAS/drone coverage typically higher; aviation/helicopter higher still. Students still need insurance (school policy or own). Confirm current figures on the CFC page before any shoot.

  • CFC insurance requirements
  • CFC permit FAQIncludes guidance on state location images / releases
  • CFC Model Filming Ordinance (PDF)Local-gov template; liability norms

City of San Jose (public property)

Visit San Jose Film Office asks for a COI to City Risk Management before filming or photography on public property (CGL and auto minimums published on their permits page; Workers’ Comp / Employers’ Liability as required by CA Labor Code). Confirm current limits and endorsement wording with Risk Management — do not treat any paraphrase as the live requirement.

  • San Jose Film Office — permits & insurance

Copyright, fair use, clearances

Fair use (17 U.S.C. § 107) is a four-factor, case-by-case doctrine — purpose/character, nature of the work, amount/substantiality, market effect — not “short clip is fine.” Music, logos, artwork, clips, and identifiable people (releases / publicity / privacy) are common clearance issues for exhibition and E&O insurance.

  • Fair Use Index
  • Circular 45 (PDF)
  • WIPO Rights Clearance — guide for independent filmmakers (PDF)

Drones — FAA Part 107 + local permit

Commercial (and most work/business) small-UAS flights generally fall under 14 CFR Part 107: Remote Pilot Certificate, aircraft registration, airspace authorization when required (LAANC / FAADroneZone), Remote ID as applicable. Local film offices (CFC, Film SF, and others) often add drone lead time and insurance on top of FAA rules. Bay Area controlled airspace (SFO, SJC, OAK) makes “just fly” illegal for commercial work.

Safety

CSATF / AMPTP bulletins · General Code · local notes

The Industry-Wide Labor-Management Safety Committee safety bulletins (hosted by CSATF) are recommended guidelines — not law. Federal, state, and local regs override. Free to download; commonly attached to call sheets and used in safety meetings. Best freely available industry-consensus safety library for U.S. sets, including small indies.

Beginner framing

  • Name a safety lead; brief the crew when conditions change.
  • Never prioritize schedule over hazard control.
  • Treat electrical, heights, traffic / picture vehicles, weapons / props, weather, and fatigue as high-risk categories.
  • Distribute relevant CSATF bulletins with the call sheet.

Gear / workflow

Brand-neutral · capture · paperwork · post

Choose tools you can operate reliably. Test the full sound + picture pipeline before shoot day. Watching films and using libraries (BAMPFA, SFFILM Education) count as craft development equal to buying gear. No brand rankings here.

Capture chain

  • Image — camera/lens capable of consistent exposure and focus.
  • Sound — dedicated recorder and/or camera audio with external mic; headphones. Prioritize sound over megapixel marketing.
  • Light — even one key + bounce beats mixed practicals alone.
  • Support — tripod or stabilization.
  • Power / media — batteries, cards, backups.
  • Continuity / slate — a simple habit that saves the edit.

Pair lighting and generators with CSATF electrical/general safety, and with local permit rules when generators sit on public property (San Jose’s inquiry form asks about generators).

Production paperwork path

  • Script / outline → shot list / schedule
  • Location permissions / permits
  • Insurance COIs / endorsements
  • Call sheet + safety bulletins
  • Releases (cast / location / music)
  • Dailies backup (2+ copies)
  • Edit → clearances check
  • Festival / export masters
  • Optional copyright registration (Circular 45)

Post & delivery

Picture edit, sound design/mix, color, titles/credits, captioning/accessibility where an exhibitor requires it, and delivery codecs per festival or distributor specs. Keep a simple asset log (camera originals, music licenses, font licenses). Exhibition readiness is part of filmmaking, not an afterthought.
